\begin{abstract}
Biological laboratories hold complementary measurements, but data-sharing constraints limit their collective use for model development. Existing local-update methods coordinate parameter fitting, whereas research agents revise computational designs; using distributed evidence at both levels requires a common interface. We introduce AI4AI4Bio, a collaborative framework coupling client-local training to an evidence-guided proposal--evaluation loop. Laboratories return model updates and aggregate development diagnostics, while a fixed research language model proposes executable configurations without receiving raw biological responses. One orchestration and selection rule connect task-specific adapters for drug--target interaction prediction, observed-response proteomic efficacy prediction and cell perturbation identification. Across retrospective held-out tests, the ten-laboratory harness reaches \StrongDTIHarness{}\% versus \StrongDTIDirect{}\% AUROC for TAPB and \StrongPTPCHarness{}\% versus \StrongPTPCDirect{}\% average precision for proteomics, relative to one-laboratory feedback-free search. Its corresponding gains on VCC single-gene, Norman double-gene and Tahoe drug identification are \StrongVCCDeltaDirect{}, \StrongNormanDeltaDirect{} and \StrongTahoeDeltaDirect{} macro Top-1 points. It is the best of the three displayed configurations on four of five endpoints. A six-arm factorial ablation shows that these gains arise primarily from additional laboratory participation: matched-access iterative feedback ties direct search on all five endpoints in the current bounded design space. AI4AI4Bio therefore provides an executable route for distributed biological evidence to improve a shared predictor and evaluate design changes, while identifying feedback-driven search as the remaining challenge.
\end{abstract}
