\begin{abstract}
Biological laboratories generate complementary experimental data, but restrictions on sharing limit their collective use for model development. We introduce AI4AI4Bio, a federated agentic research framework through which locally held measurements support both parameter learning and evidence-guided model-design revision. An inner federated process aggregates local model updates; an outer research loop converts aggregate development evidence into hypotheses, executable revisions and subsequent experiments. The research model receives no raw biological responses. We instantiate the same trainer, research interface and selection rule across drug--target interaction prediction, proteomics-based efficacy prediction and cell perturbation identification, with ten simulated laboratories per study. Our main comparison contrasts fixed models and direct optimization using one laboratory with the federated harness using ten; a separate factorial ablation isolates feedback-guided research from additional data access. On the current retrospective tests, the ProteinTalks-derived efficacy head attains \StrongPTPCHarness{}\% average precision versus \StrongPTPCDirect{}\% for single-laboratory direct optimization (\StrongPTPCDeltaDirect{} percentage points across \StrongPTPCSeeds{} seeds), while the completed TAPB DTI seed reaches \StrongDTIHarness{}\% AUROC versus \StrongDTIDirect{}\% for direct optimization. The VCC single-gene identification comparison gains \StrongVCCDeltaDirect{} Top-1 percentage points across \StrongVCCSeeds{} seeds. Current ablations attribute these gains primarily to additional laboratory participation; the DTI result currently covers one of three prespecified seeds. AI4AI4Bio provides a common interface for distributed biological evidence to guide parameter learning and iterative model improvement without centralizing raw training data.
\end{abstract}
