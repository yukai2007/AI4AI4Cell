\title{AI4AI4Bio: Federated Evidence-Guided Research for Biological Model Improvement}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
Biological laboratories generate rich experimental datasets, yet restrictions on data sharing limit their collective use for model development. We introduce AI4AI4Bio, a federated agentic research framework that enables locally held biological measurements to improve both a shared predictive model and the executable designs used to build it. An inner federated process aggregates locally trained model updates; an outer research loop uses aggregate development evidence to formulate hypotheses, revise designs, and evaluate subsequent candidates. We instantiate the same training and research protocol in three parallel case studies: drug--target interaction prediction, proteomics-based efficacy prediction, and cell perturbation identification spanning single-gene, double-gene, and drug interventions. Each study simulates ten laboratories with disjoint client datasets. Six matched configurations cross one versus ten laboratories with fixed models, feedback-free direct search, and feedback-guided research, separately measuring laboratory participation and research-loop effects. Completed first-seed development evaluations show ten-laboratory versus single-laboratory loop gains of 12.18 AP points in proteomics and 11.80, 29.30, and 13.33 Top-1 points in the three cell strata. Loop and matched-budget direct search currently tie on these primary endpoints. The corresponding three-seed held-out effects for DTI, proteomics, and cells are N/A while the unified evaluation remains incomplete. AI4AI4Bio provides a common computational interface through which distributed biological evidence can contribute to parameter learning and iterative model improvement without requiring raw response transfer during training and research.
\end{abstract}
