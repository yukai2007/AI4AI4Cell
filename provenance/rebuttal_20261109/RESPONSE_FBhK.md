# Response to Reviewer FBhK

Thank you for your careful review and for recognising the common task interface, the protocol detail and the budget-matched allocation result. We have reframed the paper around its central contribution: **collaborative model development**, in which laboratories whose data cannot be pooled jointly configure, train, evaluate and allocate training for one biological predictor using laboratory-local evidence. Section 4.3 now leads with a same-workflow comparison of ten collaborating laboratories versus one laboratory working alone, evaluated on the same held-out test. The requested same-access random and direct search controls are reported in Section 4.7 and Appendix E.1-E.2, where they characterise how proposal policies use the shared evidence. We also explain the development-test divergence (Appendix E.3) and point to the gene-disjoint grouped evaluation in Appendix E.6.

## 1. "Training data, development data, and total local computation therefore change alongside the search method" (Weakness 1)

We agree, and we now state explicitly that this is what the main comparison measures: what a laboratory gains by developing a model collaboratively instead of alone. Section 4.3 now leads with **the same Qwen history-enabled BioCoLoop workflow**, run by laboratory 0 alone or by all ten laboratories, on the same held-out tests (Appendix D, Table D.1). Collaboration improves every endpoint: DTI AUROC 89.89 to **93.76**, proteomics AP 20.13 to **36.99**, VCC Top-1 20.22 to **31.54**, Norman Top-1 14.15 to **22.25**, and Tahoe Top-1 15.42 to **22.39**. More search does not let a single laboratory close this gap: single-laboratory random, Bayesian and exhaustive-menu search all remain below collaboration on every endpoint (for example, DTI 90.10 versus 93.76).

This collaboration-versus-alone comparison on a common held-out test is the standard evaluation protocol in federated learning, used by Sheller et al. (2020), Chang et al. (2018, JAMIA), Dayan et al. (2021, EXAM), Ogier du Terrail et al. (2022, FLamby), Warnat-Herresthal et al. (2021, Swarm Learning), Saldanha et al. (2022) and, for single-cell data, Wang et al. (2024, scFed). Federated tuning studies likewise compare tuned predictors under a fixed held-out protocol (Holly et al., 2022; Wang et al., 2023, FedHPO-Bench). Section 4.2 now contains an evaluation-protocol paragraph with these citations and clarifies that every method, including BioCoLoop, returns one predictor.

## 2. Random search without replacement and direct LLM search with the same ten laboratories (Weakness 1; Question 1)

Appendix E.1-E.2 reports exactly this comparison. Random search without replacement, GP-EI, and direct and history-guided Qwen and Luna share the same ten training laboratories, ten development panels, twelve configurations, initialization and checkpoint-selection rule. Each search receives six proposal slots beyond D00, with 100 rounds per fit and a 700-round cap; random search receives the full search budget, not the fixed model's single-fit budget. Enumerating all **462** six-design subsets gives the exact expected outcome of random search with selection based only on development evidence. Failed LLM requests remain charged against the budget, and shared-first-proposal and realized-fit-count controls are also reported.

We present these controls accurately as characterising how proposal policies use shared evidence. The effect of history is task-dependent and conventional search is competitive. The clearest positive case is Norman, where Luna history reaches **23.11** Top-1 versus 22.30 for direct search and 22.66 for random search. DTI favours direct Luna and GP-EI (94.60 versus 94.27 for history), proteomics ties throughout, and on VCC the LLM searches and GP-EI retain 31.50 while random search averages 27.53. Two three-seed follow-ups show the same pattern. On the gene-disjoint cell benchmark (Appendix E.6), structured history reaches 22.888 versus 22.625 for direct and 22.668 for random search, with GP-EI at 23.152. With wider recipe choices (Appendix E.9), Luna structured history reaches DTI AUROC 94.368 versus 94.316 for random search (+0.053 points, conditional 95% interval [0.031, 0.077]), while on proteomics direct search reaches 39.017 AP, matching GP-EI. Section 4.7 now states this scope explicitly.

Regarding efficiency: we agree that the retrospective target crossings (4 versus 22 proposals on Norman/Luna, 13 versus 5 on Tahoe/Luna) are trajectory diagnostics, not realized savings, because every scheduled proposal was executed. We now describe them that way. The resource result is the separate allocation experiment you highlighted. On Norman, evidence-guided allocation improves Top-1 from 11.48 to **22.32** (+10.84, 95% CI [4.54, 17.73]) at 800 aggregation rounds and 160 development evaluations. New promotion controls (Appendix E.5) show that simpler rules recover much of this gain over uniform allocation, so we present the finding as "early collaborative evidence is useful for allocating training", not as a uniquely superior rule.

## 3. What explains the development-test divergence in the extended VCC and Tahoe searches? (Weakness 2; Question 2)

**Panel construction and weighting.** Development and test use disjoint interventions: VCC has 60/20/20 training/development/test interventions and Tahoe 180/60/67. The 20 VCC development interventions form ten panels of two, and the 60 Tahoe compounds form ten panels of six. Because every panel contains the same number of interventions and Top-1 is macro-averaged over interventions, equal weighting of panels equals the pooled intervention-macro score exactly. Equal laboratory weighting therefore does not cause the divergence, and differing query counts do not either.

**Audit.** We reconstructed all 14 selected checkpoints, re-evaluated their 140 development panels and reproduced all 80 archived held-out prefix scores (Appendix E.3). The scores are correct; the divergence arises from selection on a small development population. For example, Tahoe's +5.00 development points correspond to three additional correct development compounds, while the held-out score falls from 25.37 to 13.43.

**Roster sensitivity.** We re-scored the same initial and final checkpoints on 20 paired development-roster redraws, without retraining or reselection. Qwen/VCC's original +1.17 development advantage becomes -1.34 ± 7.37 (positive in 6/20 draws), and Tahoe's +5.00 becomes -1.33 ± 4.82 (7/20), whereas Luna/VCC remains positive in 12/20 draws per mode. The large declines therefore coincide with development gains that are sensitive to the choice of distractors. The existing trajectories do not separate repeated-selection effects from biological shift, and we state this in Appendix E.3.

**Other grouped partitions.** Appendix E.6 provides an additional grouped evaluation: four Replogle/Nadig study-cell contexts with gene-disjoint partitions of 1,208/414/406 training/development/test genes over three seeds. Here every final search exceeds the fitted D00 reference (21.43 Top-1), so the large terminal decreases do not recur; we report the one small interim reversal (seed 62, slot 2: +0.057 development, -0.257 test). Moreover, the main-setting laboratories are compound or intervention groups, not random measurements, and source-defined laboratories with one proteomic study each improve target AP from 34.41 to **37.53** and AUROC from 72.33 to **74.39** through collaboration (Appendix B.2).
