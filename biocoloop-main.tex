\begin{abstract}
Biological data are often distributed across institutions, where privacy and data-sharing constraints limit centralized access. Collaborative learning allows multiple laboratories to train a shared model without pooling raw data, but typically fixes the model structure and training hyperparameters in advance, whereas self-improving agents generally assume centralized access to training and evaluation. To address this gap, we introduce BioCoLoop, a collaborative research framework that extends collaboration from parameter fitting to model development itself. BioCoLoop uses an inner loop to train and periodically evaluate proposed model structures or training hyperparameters across laboratories, and an outer loop to use results from completed experiments to generate subsequent proposals. Across drug--target interaction prediction, observed-response proteomic efficacy prediction, and cell perturbation identification, collaborative training across laboratories consistently improves predictive performance. We further show that, with the same total amount of training and evaluation, early evaluation results can improve performance by guiding which models receive additional training. BioCoLoop therefore enables distributed biological data to contribute not only to parameter fitting, but also to the iterative improvement of the model itself.

\end{abstract}
