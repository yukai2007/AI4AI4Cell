\begin{abstract}
Biological data are often distributed across institutions, where privacy and data-sharing constraints limit centralized access. Collaborative learning can train shared models from distributed data, but typically assumes fixed model designs, while automated model development relies on centralized evaluation. To fill in this gap, we introduce BioCoLoop, a collaborative research framework that turns laboratory-local measurements into both model updates and persistent research evidence. Its harness instantiates model-development hypotheses as executable designs, evaluates them across participating laboratories, and records their outcomes to guide subsequent proposals and training allocation. Across drug--target interaction prediction, observed-response proteomic efficacy prediction, and cell perturbation identification, collaborative access consistently improves performance; the matched Qwen2.5 and GPT-5.6 Luna comparisons place BioCoLoop first on four of five primary endpoints in each block. Under matched training and evaluation budgets, trajectory-guided evidence can improve training allocation. More broadly, BioCoLoop extends collaboration from parameter fitting to model development itself, allowing laboratory-local measurements to become shared empirical evidence that steers subsequent research decisions.

\end{abstract}
