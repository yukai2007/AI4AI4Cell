\begin{abstract}
Biological model development requires both fitting parameters and choosing which configurations to investigate. We introduce BioCoLoop, a collaborative research framework connecting laboratory-local fitting to a shared record of candidate designs, development trajectories and selection decisions. Task adapters support drug--target interactions, observed-response proteomic efficacy and cell perturbation identification. Controlled studies separate three uses of collaboration: broader data access, development-history feedback and training allocation. The complete ten-laboratory workflow improves four of five primary endpoints over a single-laboratory fixed model. Across separately specified matched-access studies, model variants exceed random-search expectations in all three task families, although history-specific gains remain task-dependent and conventional search remains competitive. Selective longer fitting improves double-gene perturbation identification by 10.84 percentage points over uniform allocation at matched training and evaluation counts; simpler promotion recovers much of this gain. Compatible external studies also improve target prediction. These results establish a common experimental interface for collaborative biological model development and identify selection stability and compute-matched access controls as priorities for further improvement.
\end{abstract}
