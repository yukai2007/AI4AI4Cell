\begin{abstract}
Biological data are often distributed across laboratories that cannot pool their measurements, yet model development---choosing which configurations to train, evaluating them and deciding where to invest further training---is usually performed by a single party with centralized data. We introduce BioCoLoop, a framework that makes model development itself collaborative: laboratories fit and evaluate candidate configurations locally, and the resulting development evidence guides subsequent proposals and training allocation without pooling raw data. Task adapters support drug--target interactions, observed-response proteomic efficacy and cell perturbation identification. Following the standard federated-learning protocol, we compare collaborative development with a laboratory working alone on common held-out tests. The same BioCoLoop workflow improves all five primary endpoints when ten laboratories collaborate, raising DTI AUROC from 89.89 to 93.76 and proteomic AP from 20.13 to 36.99, and single-laboratory random, Bayesian and exhaustive search remain below the collaborative result. Laboratories defined by distinct proteomic studies raise target AP from 34.41 to 37.53. Using early collaborative evidence to allocate training improves double-gene perturbation identification by 10.84 percentage points at matched training and evaluation budgets. Matched-access controls show model variants above random-search expectations in all three task families. These results establish a practical framework through which laboratories develop stronger biological predictors together.
\end{abstract}
