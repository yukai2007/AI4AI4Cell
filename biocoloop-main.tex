\begin{abstract}
Biological data are often distributed across institutions and data holders, where privacy and data-sharing constraints limit centralized access. Using this evidence for self-improving biological AI requires improving both model parameters and the computational designs built around them. Collaborative learning typically optimizes fixed models, whereas automated model design and research generally rely on centralized training and evaluation signals. We introduce BioCoLoop, a collaborative research framework that turns laboratory-local measurements into model updates and reusable evidence for research decisions. Its harness translates hypotheses into executable designs, evaluates them at participating laboratories and packages their outcomes into a persistent research history. We study two uses of this evidence: revising subsequent proposals and allocating training to promising candidates. Across three biological settings spanning drug--target interaction prediction, observed-response proteomics, and cell perturbation, BioCoLoop with ten simulated laboratories outperforms single-laboratory fixed-model and direct-optimization baselines on four of five endpoints. Controlled comparisons establish gains from collaborative access and trajectory-guided training allocation; source-defined laboratory studies further demonstrate beneficial transfer in drug--target prediction and proteomics. BioCoLoop enables distributed biological measurements to contribute to both model training and evidence-guided model development without centralizing the raw data.
\end{abstract}
