\section*{6. Discussion}
AI4AI4Cell turns distributed biological measurements into two coupled research signals. Local training produces parameter updates for a shared predictor; local development produces evidence cards that shape the next executable proposal. The same task contract, proposal schema, experiment memory and finalizer operate across DTI, proteomics and cell perturbation, making the outer research process portable even though the biological inputs, adapters and metrics differ.

The experiments identify two complementary sources of improvement. Broader laboratory participation consistently strengthens the complete system across all five endpoints, demonstrating the value of combining distributed fitting and selection evidence. The compute-matched racing study then isolates persistent feedback from that participation effect: across 12 newly executed held-out trajectories it produces four wins, eight ties and no losses, including a +10.84-point mean Norman improvement with a positive hierarchical bootstrap interval. The DTI development replay supplies the same directional result under a slightly conservative compute comparison. Together, these results show that evidence cards are useful not only as an experiment record but also as a control signal for deciding which computational designs merit full validation.

The current benchmark establishes a reproducible starting point for that expansion. The next experimental stage can add independently collected laboratory cohorts, client rotations and same-data centralized references; broaden the editable interface from optimizer and residual-head configurations to model modules and data transformations; and combine the worker interface with secure aggregation or differential privacy. These additions preserve the central abstraction introduced here: laboratories contribute to both parameter learning and research-direction selection through a common executable evidence loop.
