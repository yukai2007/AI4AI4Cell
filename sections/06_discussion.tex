\section*{6. Discussion}
The central contribution is a common route for biological laboratories to contribute to model development at two levels: fitted parameters and evidence for revising computational designs. The framework can operate when measurements remain with their data-owning workers. Reusing one training and research implementation across DTI, proteomics and cell perturbations makes differences in outcomes attributable to task adapters and data, rather than unrelated definitions of federation or research in each task.

The completed held-out comparisons show access-enabled gains in proteomic efficacy and VCC identification. Matched-access controls reveal that the independent feedback effect is smaller and not yet reliable. This distinction informs the next experiment: improve task-grounded model interfaces and the evidence available to the controller, then evaluate the same revision across every arm. A larger language model alone is not evidence of better biological learning.

Several limits remain specific and actionable. Proteomics and cell development panels contain few positive compounds or interventions per client. The cell backbone is frozen, Tahoe uses a custom chemical adapter, and perturbation identification is not equivalent to forward-expression prediction. Laboratory partitions are simulated rather than independently collected cohorts, and the current one-laboratory reference is client 0 rather than a rotation over laboratories. Final benchmark roles used historically are not newly blinded. Strong same-data centralized references, independent-study evaluation and formal privacy mechanisms are additional validation requirements, not supplied by a positive participation contrast.
