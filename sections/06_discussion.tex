\section*{6. Discussion and limitations}
The experiments distinguish traceability, useful information and predictive gain. Cell-policy results show small full-system differences with substantial confounds, while the fixed-policy calibration replay improves gate accuracy by 4.31 percentage points over the mean single-client result and matches centralized computation (Appendix D). Neither DTI primary comparison establishes superiority. The matched PTPC study is more adverse to the intended performance claim: both primary mean differences are negative, and neural final discrimination is weak. Thus a functioning, fully recorded research loop can still select models that generalize poorly.

PTPC holds numerical selection and partner observations fixed, but it does not identify why the loop/card package underperforms in mean. A post-final descriptive audit finds identical same-seed baseline checkpoints across all arms and a decline in mean online training loss from 0.5602 to 0.04064; low final discrimination therefore coexists with successful training optimization (Appendix J). Development and visible-gate roles contain only eight and nine positives. The starting head, fixed recipe, derived features, small training pool and compound shift are plausible contributors, not measured causes. Logistic regression's stronger AUROC and lower log loss indicate room beyond the current neural search result. More calls and richer recorded evidence did not suffice to improve the primary contrasts. We retain these outcomes rather than tuning the now-observed final split.

The DTI comparison uses five campaigns per controller and short search fits followed by a fixed larger refit. It cannot establish superiority over DrugEvolve's full publication or current DTI state of the art. We did not tune the original DrugBAN domain-adaptation protocol or reconstruct undisclosed paper-specific evolved kwargs. Cold tests use exact identities, not scaffold/protein-family exclusion. PTPC uses one compound split, a derived head and simulated clients. Independent laboratory-level tests, controlled memory/hypothesis ablations and compute-normalized scaling remain necessary to establish broader utility.

The federated component provides a concrete data-flow and evidence interface, but the present biological clients are simulated shards. Partners evaluate task models and return diagnostics; they do not train task weights or update the research LLM. The PTPC factorial comparison gives direct generation the same partner access and holds training data fixed. It tests research organization and a richer client-resolved diagnostic package, not federation alone or the separate contribution of each extra statistic. Actual institutional participation, privacy accounting and malicious-client behavior require separate studies. Harness-free and DrugEvolve can also be connected to local-data interfaces. The defensible motivation is that artifact contracts and evidence lineage support inspectable coordination under measurement-sharing constraints, not that competing controllers are fundamentally unable to preserve data locality.

Observed protein responses can contain strong information about efficacy, so success on PTPC must not be interpreted as predicting an unseen drug response from an untreated cell. The ProteinTalks-derived head and public subdataset are an explicitly bounded adaptation \citep{Sun2026ProteinTalks}; they do not substitute for evaluating the full virtual-cell model. More generally, an auditable proposal history can document what happened without proving that the proposed biological explanation caused the measured change.
