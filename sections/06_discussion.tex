\section*{6. Discussion: what improves the biological predictor?}
The principal gains come from predictive design and data availability. Improved retrieval and proteomics features raise scores, while compact prediction programs transfer across backbones without renewed search. Sharing improved starts with both controllers separates these effects from research organization. Native trajectories show how measured evidence influences executable revisions.

Federation expands the measurements available to fit a shared design. Ten-client proteomics improves AP and AUROC while reproducing the centralized objective. Direct generation can use the same service, so matched-access comparisons are essential. The new drug pilot likewise favors ten-client access, but fixed and loop heads tie in Top-1. Simulated clients test this computation, not institutional participation incentives.

Evaluation quality remains a bottleneck. Proteomics' initial development/gate roles contain eight and nine positives; training-only CV covers 86. Controller rankings vary across these studies and metrics. The cell pilots have only two or six local development interventions and do not establish head-training convergence; VCC remains below chance. Multi-seed client rotations, stronger biological fine-tuning and independent-study tests are needed. A matched gate ablation would separately test the retention rule.

Task boundaries also matter: BAN and TAPB differ in representation budget, exact-identity coldness does not establish scaffold/family generalization, and PTPC classifies observed-response efficacy rather than reproducing ProteinTalks \citep{Sun2026ProteinTalks}. Local execution supports collaboration; formal privacy and independent institutional deployment require additional mechanisms and validation.
