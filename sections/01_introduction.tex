\section*{1. Introduction}
Biological laboratories hold complementary measurements of molecular interactions, protein responses and cellular perturbations. Combining these observations could improve predictive models, but unrestricted transfer may conflict with data ownership, participant protection or unpublished research. Biological collaboration must also accommodate study and platform variation \citep{Bakhtiari2025FedscGen}. Multi-institutional medical learning illustrates the value of collaborative training without centralizing patient data \citep{Sheller2020}. The corresponding opportunity in biological research is not only to fit better parameters, but also to use distributed experimental feedback to improve the computational designs being fitted.

Predictive models learn from accessible measurements, while agentic research systems turn hypotheses into executable changes and evaluate their consequences \citep{Jiang2025AIDE,Lu2024AIScientist,Zhou2026DrugEvolve}. Connecting these capabilities across laboratories requires two interfaces: a way to train a shared biological model locally, and a way to return useful evidence to the research process without putting private biological responses into the language model's context.

AI4AI4Bio couples these interfaces. An inner federated process distributes a common model to participating laboratories and aggregates local updates. An outer loop proposes a testable design revision, executes a complete training schedule, compares aggregate development evidence and retains or rejects the candidate. Both successful and unsuccessful trials inform subsequent proposals. The research language model remains fixed; the objects being improved are the biological predictor's parameters and its executable configuration. Saved hypotheses, configurations, training receipts and selection decisions make the process inspectable.

We evaluate three parallel task families: drug--target interaction (DTI), observed-response proteomic efficacy classification, and five-option cell perturbation identification. The cell family contains single-gene, double-gene and drug perturbations. All use the same federated core, proposer, candidate library, budget and retention rule, with task-specific prediction and scoring adapters. Six arms cross one versus ten simulated laboratories with fixed, direct-search and loop-enabled training. This distinguishes a gain from more accessible private data from a gain due to feedback-guided research.

Our contributions are (i) a two-level framework that connects client-local biological training to evidence-guided design revision; (ii) a consistent implementation across three biological task families; and (iii) a benchmark protocol that separately tests laboratory participation, design search and iterative feedback. The current draft reports completed development runs alongside explicit N/A entries for unfinished evaluations. Earlier source-search and held-out studies remain separately labeled supporting evidence, rather than being substituted into the new uniform comparison.
