\section*{1. Introduction}
Biological model development must turn proposed mechanisms into executable changes, test them against observations, and use the results to guide the next design. Complementary measurements may be held by institutions with different ownership and sharing constraints. Biological collaboration must also accommodate study and platform variation, while expression profiles and unpublished findings can make unrestricted sharing undesirable \citep{Bakhtiari2025FedscGen}. Multi-institutional medical learning illustrates the value of collaboration without centralizing patient data \citep{Sheller2020}. Can locally evaluated evidence similarly improve a shared biological model design without putting the underlying measurements into the research model's context?

AI4AI4Cell addresses this question through hypothesis-driven research on executable artifacts. A fixed language model proposes a targeted change and its expected effect, implements it within a task interface, and tests it with a fixed evaluator. Measurements determine retention; successful and failed attempts inform later proposals. The object being improved is the biological model or policy, not the language model's weights. Building on iterative code generation and experimental feedback \citep{Jiang2025AIDE,Lu2024AIScientist,Zhou2026DrugEvolve}, we connect model-design decisions to locally held biological evidence.

Partners contribute at two levels: local trainers fit task weights through objective and gradient aggregation, and local evaluators return evidence for the next source revision. The shared research output is executable design: it can be fitted against participating clients' measurements or reused with another compatible predictor. Evaluation summaries connect local computation to subsequent proposals without sending raw measurements to the research LLM. Linked source changes and measured consequences make each design decision inspectable.

Our contributions are a common hypothesis-to-code interface for biological model improvement; its integration with actual client-local task training; and controlled evaluations separating predictive design, data availability and research organization. Task adapters cover DTI interaction modules and prediction programs, proteomics representations, and exploratory cell policies. Fixed predictive references, harness-free generation and native research systems test whether improvements come from a stronger predictor, additional local data, or the research loop itself. Complete performance and cost records support this attribution.
