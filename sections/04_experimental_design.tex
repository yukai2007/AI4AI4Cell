\section*{4. Experimental design}
\subsection*{4.1 Three questions: parameters, proposals, and generalization}
Each case tests whether distributed measurements improve a shared predictor at both the parameter and design levels, and whether retained changes generalize beyond the local setting that produced them. We assign training and development groups to ten disjoint simulated laboratories, balancing by compounds or intervention identities rather than equal cell counts. A single-laboratory method accesses only preselected client 0; a ten-laboratory method uses all ten clients. Within a task, every arm uses the same scorer and candidate rosters.

Three complementary contrasts address the two-level question. Ten- versus one-laboratory training, with research mode fixed, measures the value of broader laboratory participation through additional training data and development evidence. Loop versus fixed measures the complete research workflow. To isolate iterative feedback, the compute-matched racing study fixes the ten-design slate, ten-laboratory access, total client-epoch budget and number of development measurements, varying only whether early evidence reallocates later validation compute. After development selection, a common held-out scorer measures transfer of the selected predictor.

The main operational table reports a fixed model and direct optimizer with one laboratory against our complete ten-laboratory harness. A factorial ablation contains all six combinations of one/ten laboratories and fixed/direct/loop training; seeds 42, 43 and 44 are shared across these arms. ``Fixed'' denotes a fixed architecture and optimization recipe whose predictive parameters still receive the complete training schedule. The feedback-isolation study uses newly executed seeds 53, 54 and 55 for proteomics and cell perturbation. Its direct arm trains all ten candidates for 80 rounds; its loop screens all ten for 20 rounds and fully validates six promoted candidates for 100 rounds, giving both arms 800 full-client rounds and 160 development measurements.

Selection uses participating clients' development data. After selection, every arm is evaluated on the same ten-client diagnostic panel and frozen held-out test set. Equal-client AUROC/AP gives each laboratory the same influence; the cell clients have equal development-intervention counts, so their mean macro score equals the overall intervention-macro score. Tables aggregate all three prespecified seeds and report sample SD. Checkpoint, configuration and data hashes bind each score to its selected run. \subsection*{4.2 Drug--target interaction prediction}
DTI predicts a binary interaction from a compound and a protein sequence. We use the released BindingDB training/development split of 50,149/5,604 pairs. Canonical-compound groups are assigned to clients with a label-independent size-balancing rule, producing compound-disjoint clients while preserving the source split within each client.

The task reference uses the released TAPB architecture with frozen public ESM2 protein features and freshly initialized drug, fusion and classification modules \citep{lin2025tapb}. A target dictionary derived from client 0's training proteins is shared identically by every arm, ensuring a common initialization. The objective combines TAPB's classification NLL and masked-language loss under the shared batch-64 training pipeline. Candidate designs can add a zero-output residual adapter before TAPB's native confounder-averaged prediction.

Selection uses mean-client AUROC; AP, MCC, accuracy at 0.5 and binary cross-entropy are secondary. The fixed test suite contains 5,505 random, 3,791 unseen-drug and 2,507 unseen-protein pairs. Random-split AUROC is the main-table endpoint, and both unseen-entity endpoints appear in the complete results. All six arms and three seeds complete the full protocol.

DrugBAN supplies an additional architecture reference under the uniform multi-laboratory trainer \citep{Bai2023DrugBAN,DrugBANCode}.

\subsection*{4.3 Proteomics-based efficacy prediction}
The public ProteinTalks PTPC supplement provides measured proteomic responses and binary efficacy labels \citep{Sun2026ProteinTalks}. The task predicts efficacy from a treated condition's observed 6h/24h proteome and drug representation. We partition the 491-condition source pool, spanning 333 compounds, into 386 fitting and 105 development conditions by compound group. Ten clients each own 49--50 total conditions, with compound-disjoint training and development groups and both classes in every local development set. Local fitting sets contain 35--42 conditions.

The task reference is a ProteinTalks-derived observed-response efficacy head: protein and drug convolutional branches followed by a 64--32--1 classifier, trained from random initialization on observed 6h/24h responses and Morgan features. A 24h linear/Morgan model provides a complementary non-neural reference.

Average precision (AP) is primary; AUROC, binary cross-entropy and accuracy at 0.5 are secondary. The 148-condition test role is evaluated after the development-selected checkpoint is frozen.

\subsection*{4.4 Cell perturbation identification}
Given an observed expression response and five candidate interventions, the model selects the true perturbation by cosine agreement with predicted responses. The three strata cover VCC single-gene perturbations, Norman double-gene overexpression, and Tahoe drugs at a fixed cell context and dose. Whole-intervention train/development/test counts are respectively 60/20/20, 70/20/27 and 180/60/67. Each laboratory owns six/seven/eighteen training interventions and two/two/six development interventions. Assignment balances intervention identities, while retaining the naturally varying number of VCC cells per intervention.

The public CELLxGENE-pretrained scDEBART backbone is frozen and its response MLP is trainable \citep{sung2026scdebart}. Three upstream attention masks are implemented as additive blocking masks and verified by response-free synthetic tests. This correction leaves pretrained weights, targets, splits and candidate rosters unchanged; features are then re-extracted for every arm.

Genetic tasks use the released perturbation encoding and exclude directly perturbed genes from loss and ranking coordinates. Tahoe adds fixed Morgan-projection conditioning for chemical identities. The uniform cell objective combines response MSE with 0.1 times a contrastive loss formed from local training conditions.

Primary scoring is intervention-macro Top-1, with a 20\% random-choice expectation; micro Top-1, MRR, Top-3 and cosine-logit cross-entropy are secondary. All arms receive the same five candidate interventions for each response.
