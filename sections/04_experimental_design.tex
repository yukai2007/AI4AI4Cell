\section*{4. Experimental design}
We separate three questions: whether a better executable predictor transfers to held-out examples; whether access to additional local training data helps; and whether iterative research improves over direct generation with the same access. All arms within each task use the same unchanged scorer. Different task metrics are not averaged into an overall score.

\subsection*{4.1 Drug--target interaction prediction}
The DTI task predicts binary BindingDB interactions on the original DrugEvolve random, unseen-drug and unseen-protein test splits, containing 5,505, 3,791 and 2,507 pairs. Coldness is exact drug/protein-string identity, not scaffold or sequence-homology separation. We pin DrugBAN and DrugEvolve to their released implementations.

\paragraph{Full-training architecture transfer.}
Five historical campaigns per controller compare direct generation, native DrugEvolve and our loop from identical DrugBAN source: ceilings of three candidates, 24 calls and four fits including baseline, using 2,048 training rows and three epochs. We transfer \emph{all fifteen} selected sources, without test-based filtering. Fixed DrugBAN and released DrugBAN-Evo complete the five-method roster.

All BAN-family fits use 50,149 training pairs, 5,604 validation pairs, 100 epochs, batch size 64, Adam at $5\times10^{-5}$ and FP32. Paired seeds 342--346 share encoder/classifier initialization. The earliest strict maximum validation AUROC selects the checkpoint after 100 epochs. Four same-source/seed/recipe aliases reduce 25 labels to 21 physical fits; different seeds never share weights. TAPB \citep{lin2025tapb} adds a fixed reference with frozen ESM2-650M features and its released training recipe on identical data, not a BAN-capacity-matched controller. Cosine 5-NN uses the full training pool. DTI tests predictive designs and controllers, not federated training (recipes and assets: Appendix L).

\paragraph{Fixed-predictor program research.}
A no-refit experiment gives both controllers the same DrugBAN342 fit, cosine predictor and mixture $0.7p_{\rm BAN}+0.3p_{\rm cosine}$. Five trajectories each revise a stateless combination function, with ceilings of three candidates and 24 calls. Retention requires AUROC gains above $10^{-4}$ on both compound-hash validation halves. All ten selections close before testing. Frozen transfer substitutes TAPB probabilities, mapping trajectory indices 542--546 to backbone seeds 342--346 without changing source, cosine input or manual rule. This tests portability without additional fitting or search (Appendix M).

\subsection*{4.2 Observed-response proteomics and local training}
The public ProteinTalks PTPC supplement \citep{Sun2026ProteinTalks} supplies measured 6h/24h proteomes and binary efficacy labels. The admitted data contain 979 conditions, 666 compounds and 5,171 proteins. Compound-grouped train/development/visible-gate/partner/final roles contain 491/96/90/154/148 conditions. The final role has 22 positives. This task classifies efficacy \emph{after observing} the two proteomic profiles; it does not forecast untreated-cell responses or reproduce the complete ProteinTalks model.

Twenty campaigns cross direct generation and the native loop with one or ten clients at five compound partitions, 442--446. Both controllers receive the same rows within each client-count condition: 44--59 for one client and all 491 for ten. Research changes a row-wise NumPy feature map, starting from locally normalized protein/compound blocks. All candidates use Section 3's zero-initialized, $C=1$ local-gradient logistic fitter. Ceilings are three candidate opportunities, four fit attempts including baseline and 24 LLM calls; retention requires both development and visible-gate AP gains above $10^{-4}$. All selections close before final scoring. Fixed logistic and same-pool cosine references separate data access from research organization. The partner role is unused (complete roster and equivalence check: Appendix K).

\paragraph{Training-CV-guided revision.}
The initial selector sees eight development and nine gate positives. A separately frozen revision uses compound-grouped three-fold CV within the 491 training rows, covering 86 positives. Direct, loop and native Helmsman each receive eight trajectories (642--649), sharing the ten-client pool, fitter, baseline, agenda, budgets and selector. Retention requires pooled OOF AP and minimum-fold AP gains above $10^{-4}$. Helmsman preserves its native stop at first valid execution; unused opportunities remain reported. All 24 selections close before full-training deployment and final scoring (Appendix O).

\subsection*{4.3 Cell perturbation identification and statistical scope}
New cell pilots identify single-gene, double-gene and drug perturbations from five options using trainable scDEBART heads. Ten-client FedAvg and local Qwen direct/loop comparisons share an intervention-macro Top-1 scorer; Appendix Q reports all splits, adapters, secondary metrics and one-seed results.
\paragraph{Matched strong-start follow-up.}
A researcher-designed training/development search improves the DTI retrieval mixture and PTPC row-local features before a new controller comparison. These starting designs are shared identically, not credited as native loop discoveries. Five direct/loop pairs per task (742--746) retain the original APIs, selectors, scorers and ceilings: three candidate opportunities, four evaluations including baseline and 24 LLM calls; PTPC permits 12 fold fits. DTI sources research one fixed TAPB fit and deploy to seeds 342--346 by a fixed mapping. PTPC uses the same three training folds and local-gradient fitter. All twenty selections close before final deployment. Separately, the unchanged improved PTPC map is fitted with one, three and ten clients at each of five fixed partitions. Appendix P gives the engineering search, complete matched results, costs and non-improving alternatives.

Unseen-drug AUROC is primary for DTI; AP is primary for proteomics. DTI also reports AP, MCC, accuracy at 0.5 and BCE (Appendix L); proteomics reports AUROC, BCE and accuracy at 0.5. AP is average precision, not trapezoidal PR-AUC. Higher is better except BCE. Cell-policy metrics are Top-1, AUC3 and AURB3 (Appendix I); the latter two are three-step search scores, not ROC-AUC. Predeclared paired contrasts use exact two-sided sign-flip tests and 10,000-draw paired bootstrap intervals, with Holm correction within two-contrast families. Five-pair exact tests cannot attain $p<0.05$. Native controllers share ceilings, not prompts or realized costs.

These are disclosed developmental evaluations on public test roles that were previously exposed, with each new study's source selections closed before reuse. They are not newly blinded validation.
