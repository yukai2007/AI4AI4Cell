\section*{4. Experimental design}
\subsection*{4.1 Three questions: parameters, proposals, and generalization}
Each case tests whether distributed measurements improve a shared predictor at both the parameter and design levels, and whether retained changes generalize beyond the local setting that produced them. We assign training and development groups to ten disjoint simulated laboratories, balancing by compounds or intervention identities rather than equal cell counts. A single-laboratory method accesses only preselected client 0; a ten-laboratory method uses all ten clients. Within a task, every arm uses the same scorer and candidate rosters.

Three contrasts address the two-level question. Ten- versus one-laboratory training, with the research mode held fixed, measures access-enabled improvement: it expands both training data and development evidence, and increases work per round. It does not isolate a parameter-only mechanism or compare with same-data centralized training. Loop versus direct search, at matched participation, Qwen checkpoint, design menu, six candidate slots and training schedule, tests whether evaluation history helps proposal generation. Direct receives no evaluation outcomes in its prompts, although its final candidate is selected by the same development scorer; it is distinct from a no-LLM fixed predictor. Held-out tests assess generalization after selection. A separate common development panel diagnoses every arm without returning its scores to the proposer.

Loop minus fixed measures the complete research workflow. The main operational table reports a fixed model and direct optimizer with one laboratory against our harness with ten, an access-enabled system comparison rather than an equal-data algorithm comparison. A separate ablation includes all six combinations of one/ten laboratories and fixed/direct/loop training. ``Fixed'' means a fixed architecture and training recipe, not frozen predictive parameters: it receives the complete training schedule. Seeds 42, 43 and 44 are specified for all tasks, without selecting favorable seeds.

Selection uses only participating clients' development data. After selection, all arms are diagnosed on the same ten-client panel, which is not returned to the proposer. Equal-client AUROC/AP is not pooled AUROC/AP; cell clients have equal development-intervention counts, so their mean macro score equals the overall intervention-macro score. Tables aggregate every completed prespecified seed, report sample SD when at least two seeds are available, and keep missing evaluations as N/A. Checkpoint, configuration and data hashes are recorded before test-response access. These endpoints are retrospective held-out scores, not newly blinded confirmation. Tests never feed back to the proposer. Bold marks displayed maxima, including ties, not significance or global SOTA.

\subsection*{4.2 Drug--target interaction prediction}
DTI predicts a binary interaction from a compound and a protein sequence. We preserve the released DrugEvolve training/development split: 50,149/5,604 BindingDB pairs. Canonical-compound groups are assigned to clients using a size-balancing rule without using labels. Clients are compound-disjoint; the original row-level train/development split can contain a compound in both roles within its owning client. This development endpoint is not a cold-compound benchmark.

The main reference uses the released TAPB architecture with frozen public ESM2 protein features and freshly initialized drug, fusion and classification modules \citep{lin2025tapb}. The target dictionary is derived only from client 0's training proteins and shared identically by every arm. No pooled target-trained checkpoint initializes a one-client model. The objective retains TAPB's classification NLL and masked-language loss; batch 64, round-wise optimizer resets and the common learning-rate library are explicit adaptations to our shared protocol, not a reproduction of the authors' centralized training recipe. A zero-output residual augments its classifier before the native confounder-averaged prediction.

Selection uses mean client AUROC; AP, MCC, accuracy at 0.5 and binary cross-entropy are secondary. The fixed test suite contains 5,505 random, 3,791 unseen-drug and 2,507 unseen-protein pairs. Random-split AUROC is the main-table endpoint, with both unseen-entity endpoints mandatory secondary reports; the endpoint is not selected by outcome. All six arms and all three prespecified seeds have completed selection and held-out scoring on these endpoints. Exact-identity coldness does not imply scaffold or protein-family separation.

DrugBAN supplies an additional native-model multi-laboratory comparison \citep{Bai2023DrugBAN,DrugBANCode}. Native DrugEvolve, released DrugBAN-Evo, centralized TAPB and cosine results from earlier full-training protocols remain in Appendices L, M and P; they are not inserted as new uniform-protocol results.

\subsection*{4.3 Proteomics-based efficacy prediction}
The public ProteinTalks PTPC supplement provides measured proteomic responses and binary efficacy labels \citep{Sun2026ProteinTalks}. This task predicts efficacy after observing a treated condition's proteome; it does not predict a proteome from an untreated cell or reproduce the complete ProteinTalks system. The new uniform development protocol uses only the historical 491-condition training pool, spanning 333 compounds, and reserves compound groups internally: 386 conditions for fitting and 105 for development. Ten clients each own 49--50 total conditions, with compound-disjoint training and development groups and both classes in each local development set. Local fitting sets contain 35--42 conditions.

The main model is a ProteinTalks-derived observed-response efficacy head: protein and drug convolutional branches followed by a 64--32--1 classifier, trained from random initialization on observed 6h/24h responses and Morgan features. This is an efficacy-head adaptation, not the complete ppODE trajectory model. A separately reported 24h linear/Morgan control preserves a simpler non-neural reference and is never relabeled as ProteinTalks.

AP is primary; AUROC, binary cross-entropy and accuracy at 0.5 are secondary. AP denotes average precision rather than trapezoidal PR-AUC. The 148-condition historical final role is evaluated only after freezing development-selected checkpoints. Its pooled test AP and the mean-client development AP are distinct endpoints.

\subsection*{4.4 Cell perturbation identification}
Given an observed expression response and five candidate interventions, the model selects the true perturbation by cosine agreement with predicted responses. Cell studies retain three strata: VCC single-gene perturbations, Norman double-gene overexpression, and Tahoe drugs at a fixed cell context and dose. Whole-intervention train/development/test counts are respectively 60/20/20, 70/20/27 and 180/60/67. Each laboratory owns six/seven/eighteen training interventions and two/two/six development interventions. This is intervention-balanced simulation; VCC raw-cell counts are not equal across clients. Appendix Q details source datasets, grouping and public controls.

The public CELLxGENE-pretrained scDEBART backbone is frozen and its response MLP is trainable \citep{sung2026scdebart}. A separately versioned adaptation corrects three upstream Boolean attention masks to additive blocking masks, verified by response-free synthetic tests, without changing pretrained weights, targets, splits or rosters; features are re-extracted and every arm is rerun. The uncorrected implementation remains a diagnostic control.

Genetic tasks use the released perturbation encoding and exclude directly perturbed genes from loss and ranking coordinates. Tahoe adds fixed Morgan-projection conditioning because the released genetic model does not natively encode drugs. The uniform cell loss is response MSE plus 0.1 times local-training-condition contrastive loss. Neither development nor test responses supply contrastive negatives.

Primary scoring is intervention-macro Top-1, with expected random Top-1 of 20\%; micro Top-1, MRR, Top-3 and cosine-logit cross-entropy are secondary. All arms share the same five options. This is response-based identification, not a forward-expression leaderboard comparison.
