\section*{4. Experimental design}
\subsection*{4.1 Three parallel cases: main comparison and attribution}
Each case asks whether distributed biological observations improve a shared predictor and whether iterative feedback further improves its research process. We establish task-specific partitions, then assign training and development groups to ten disjoint simulated laboratories. Balancing uses compounds or intervention identities, not necessarily equal numbers of underlying cells. A single-laboratory method accesses only preselected client 0; a federated method uses all ten clients. Within a task, every arm uses the same scorer and candidate rosters.

The main table has three configurations: fixed model (one laboratory), direct optimization or harness-free control (one laboratory), and our harness (ten laboratories). ``Fixed'' means a fixed architecture and training recipe, not frozen predictive parameters: it receives the complete training schedule. The main contrast measures the joint system and data-access benefit, not an equal-data algorithm comparison. A separate ablation includes all six combinations of one/ten laboratories and fixed/direct/loop training. Direct receives the same Qwen checkpoint, design menu, six candidate slots and full-training schedule, but no evaluation history. It is distinct from a fixed predictor with no LLM. Seeds 42, 43 and 44 are specified for all tasks, without selecting favorable seeds.

We distinguish three contrasts: loop minus fixed measures the complete research workflow; loop minus direct isolates feedback-guided iteration at matched access and budget; and ten-laboratory loop minus one-laboratory loop measures additional private-data participation. The latter changes accessible sample size and is not evidence that federated optimization beats centralized training on the same data. Fixed and direct participation contrasts are also retained.

\subsection*{4.2 Drug--target interaction prediction}
DTI predicts a binary interaction from a compound and a protein sequence. We preserve the released DrugEvolve training/development split: 50,149/5,604 BindingDB pairs. Canonical-compound groups are assigned to clients using a size-balancing rule without using labels. Clients are compound-disjoint; the original row-level train/development split can contain a compound in both roles within its owning client. This development endpoint is not a cold-compound benchmark.

The main reference uses the released TAPB architecture with frozen public ESM2 protein features and freshly initialized drug, fusion and classification modules \citep{lin2025tapb}. The target dictionary is derived only from client 0's training proteins and shared identically by every arm. No pooled target-trained checkpoint initializes a one-client model. The objective retains TAPB's classification NLL and masked-language loss; batch 64, round-wise optimizer resets and the common learning-rate library are explicit adaptations to our shared protocol, not a reproduction of the authors' centralized training recipe. A zero-output residual augments its classifier before the native confounder-averaged prediction.

Selection uses mean client AUROC; AP, MCC, accuracy at 0.5 and binary cross-entropy are secondary. The fixed test suite contains 5,505 random, 3,791 unseen-drug and 2,507 unseen-protein pairs. Random-split AUROC is the main-table endpoint, with both unseen-entity endpoints mandatory secondary reports; the endpoint is not selected by outcome. Corresponding entries remain N/A until all six arms finish selection and scoring. Exact-identity coldness does not imply scaffold or protein-family separation.

DrugBAN supplies an additional native-model federated comparison \citep{Bai2023DrugBAN,DrugBANCode}. Native DrugEvolve, released DrugBAN-Evo, centralized TAPB and cosine results from earlier full-training protocols remain in Appendices L, M and P. They are not inserted as new federated results. Recent model-family provenance justifies the reference choice but does not by itself establish SOTA on these client-restricted tasks.

\subsection*{4.3 Proteomics-based efficacy prediction}
The public ProteinTalks PTPC supplement provides measured proteomic responses and binary efficacy labels \citep{Sun2026ProteinTalks}. This task predicts efficacy after observing a treated condition's proteome; it does not predict a proteome from an untreated cell or reproduce the complete ProteinTalks system. The new uniform development protocol uses only the historical 491-condition training pool, spanning 333 compounds, and reserves compound groups internally: 386 conditions for fitting and 105 for development. Ten clients each own 49--50 total conditions, with compound-disjoint training and development groups and both classes in each local development set. Local fitting sets contain 35--42 conditions.

The main model is a ProteinTalks-derived observed-response efficacy head: protein and drug convolutional branches followed by a 64--32--1 classifier, trained from random initialization on observed 6h/24h responses and Morgan features. The baseline has 464,193 parameters. This is an efficacy-head adaptation, not the complete ppODE trajectory model. All six arms share inputs and initialization; unfinished seed evaluations remain N/A. A separately reported 24h linear/Morgan control preserves a simpler non-neural reference and is never relabeled as ProteinTalks.

AP is primary; AUROC, binary cross-entropy and accuracy at 0.5 are secondary. AP denotes average precision rather than trapezoidal PR-AUC. The 148-condition historical final role is evaluated only after freezing development-selected checkpoints. Its pooled test AP and the mean-client development AP are distinct endpoints.

\subsection*{4.4 Cell perturbation identification}
Given an observed expression response and five candidate interventions, the model selects the true perturbation by cosine agreement with predicted responses. Cell studies retain three strata: VCC single-gene perturbations, Norman double-gene overexpression, and Tahoe drugs at a fixed cell context and dose. Whole-intervention train/development/test counts are respectively 60/20/20, 70/20/27 and 180/60/67. Each laboratory owns six/seven/eighteen training interventions and two/two/six development interventions. This is intervention-balanced simulation; VCC raw-cell counts are not equal across clients. Appendix Q details source datasets, grouping and public controls.

The public CELLxGENE-pretrained scDEBART backbone is frozen and its response MLP is trainable \citep{sung2026scdebart}. A separately versioned adaptation corrects three upstream Boolean attention masks to additive blocking masks, verified by response-free synthetic tests. It changes no pretrained weights, response targets, splits or rosters; features are re-extracted and every arm is rerun. The uncorrected implementation remains a diagnostic control, not silently mixed with corrected-model results.

Genetic tasks use the released perturbation encoding and exclude directly perturbed genes from loss and ranking coordinates. Tahoe adds fixed Morgan-projection conditioning because the released genetic model does not natively encode drugs. The uniform cell loss is response MSE plus 0.1 times local-training-condition contrastive loss. Neither development nor test responses supply contrastive negatives.

Primary scoring is intervention-macro Top-1. Micro Top-1, mean reciprocal rank (MRR), Top-3 and cosine-logit cross-entropy at temperature 0.1 are secondary; uniform random choice has expected Top-1 of 20\%. All arms share the same five options per query. This is response-based intervention identification, not a forward-expression leaderboard comparison.

\subsection*{4.5 Evaluation roles and reporting}
Candidate selection uses only participating clients' development data. After selection, all arms are diagnosed on the same ten-client development panel; this report is not returned to the proposer. Equal-client AUROC/AP is not pooled AUROC/AP. Cell clients have equal numbers of development interventions, so their mean macro score equals the overall intervention-macro score.

Tables give the number of completed seeds for each task and aggregate every completed prespecified seed. Sample standard deviation is reported when at least two seeds are available; a one-seed value is not a three-seed estimate. Missing studies remain N/A. Before test-response access, the evaluator records selected checkpoint, configuration, source, data and scorer hashes. Earlier studies examined these benchmark roles, so this is retrospective held-out evaluation, not newly blinded confirmation. Tests never feed back to the proposer. We retain all failures and loss directions and do not average AUROC, AP and Top-1 into an overall score. Bold marks all best displayed values, including ties, not significance or global SOTA.
