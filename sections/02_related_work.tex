\section*{2. Related work}
\paragraph{Collaborative learning for biological data.}
Parameter aggregation enables institutions to train a shared model while retaining their measurements locally \citep{McMahan2017,Sheller2020}. Its benefit is conventionally measured by comparing collaborative training with each site training alone under a common held-out evaluation \citep{Sheller2020,Chang2018Distributed,Dayan2021FederatedCOVID,Terrail2022FLamby,WarnatHerresthal2021Swarm,Saldanha2022Swarm}, with clients constructed through synthetic dataset partitions \citep{McMahan2017,He2020FedML} or natural entity partitions \citep{Caldas2018LEAF}; we adopt this protocol, using grouped synthetic partitions in the main study and study-defined laboratories in the source-transfer experiments. In single-cell analysis, scFed addresses cell-type classification and FedscGen addresses distributed batch correction \citep{Wang2024scFed,Bakhtiari2025FedscGen}. Tabula and its Chiron implementation support collaborative single-cell foundation-model training through an agent-enabled platform \citep{Ding2026Tabula,ChironCode}. Automated design can interact with local training: FedEx tunes hyperparameters while training shared weights \citep{Khodak2021FedEx}, FedNAS searches architectures over distributed data \citep{He2020FedNAS}, and federated tuning is evaluated on held-out data after distributed validation \citep{Holly2022FederatedHPO}, with FedHPO-Bench providing a benchmark suite for such methods \citep{Wang2023FedHPOBench}. FedEx and FedNAS optimize a predefined numerical search space with a fixed algorithm; BioCoLoop instead lets a language-model agent propose typed design changes with stated hypotheses, returns per-laboratory development evidence to that agent, and allocates training among its proposals, so that the same interface serves different task models and research agents.

\paragraph{Self-improving agents in biological research.}
Self-improving agents turn experiment outcomes into subsequent computational proposals. AIDE searches candidate programs through drafting, debugging and improvement \citep{Jiang2025AIDE}, while the AI Scientist and AI-Scientist-v2 connect ideas to executed experiments, replanning, tree search and writing \citep{Lu2024AIScientist,Yamada2025AIScientistV2}. DrugEvolve combines research, implementation, evaluation and algorithm memory for drug-development tasks \citep{Zhou2026DrugEvolve,DrugEvolveCode}. BioCoLoop studies how multiple data-owning laboratories can supply development feedback through a shared research interface. TAPB, a ProteinTalks-derived efficacy head and scDEBART-based cell representations provide its task-specific prediction components \citep{lin2025tapb,Sun2026ProteinTalks,sung2026scdebart}. The proposal schema, fitting service and selection mechanism are common across tasks. Strong simple references on some perturbation benchmarks motivate evaluating the research procedure with fixed task predictors \citep{ahlmann2025linear}. Configuration search also connects to random search \citep{bergstra2012random}, Bayesian optimization \citep{snoek2012practical} and language-model proposal distributions \citep{yang2024opro}. We use feedback-free direct search to isolate the effect of development history under a shared proposal and fitting interface.

\paragraph{Collaborative scientific agents.}
The Virtual Biotech organizes specialist AI agents to integrate biomedical evidence for therapeutic discovery \citep{Zhang2026VirtualBiotech}. AutoScientists uses self-organizing agent teams with shared experimental state and recorded successes and failures \citep{Gao2026AutoScientists}. In both, the collaborators are agents sharing one data view. Helmsman, the closest precedent, uses LLM agents to generate and refine federated-learning systems in simulation \citep{Li2025Helmsman}. In BioCoLoop the collaborators are data-holding laboratories and the research loop itself is federated: candidates are fitted and evaluated locally, and shared development evidence drives the next proposals and training allocation.
