\section*{2. Related work and comparison scope}
AIDE stores candidate programs in a solution tree, using drafting, debugging and improvement operators conditioned on summarized evaluations \citep{Jiang2025AIDE}. The original AI Scientist connects idea generation to executed experiments, notes, replanning and paper writing \citep{Lu2024AIScientist}. Building on these established research-loop ideas, we study a common executable contract for local biological evidence and measure the effects of workflow and client-resolved diagnostics.

DrugEvolve combines research, implementation, debugging, evaluation, analysis, memory, and algorithm lineage across drug-development tasks \citep{Zhou2026DrugEvolve,DrugEvolveCode}. Its manuscript reports 11 task families and 120 test sets, including external, cold, temporal, or structural evaluations where appropriate. Our DTI comparison uses its released Creation pipeline and local database with explicit model-transport and evaluator adaptations. The separately released DrugBAN-Evo module is an algorithm reference, distinct from rerunning the DrugEvolve controller.

DrugBAN represents drugs as molecular graphs and proteins as sequences, and uses bilinear attention to model local interactions \citep{Bai2023DrugBAN,DrugBANCode}. We reconstruct its full encoder--interaction--classifier model using the pinned original implementation, then restrict search to the BAN component. We use the non-domain-adaptation training configuration and a deliberately limited data and compute budget. Our numbers therefore do not reproduce the published full-budget DrugBAN or DrugEvolve scores.

Federated averaging aggregates locally computed parameter updates \citep{McMahan2017}. Our clients instead contribute evaluation and diagnostic evidence that informs updates to executable policies or task-model designs. The research LLM stays fixed, and the common task trainer fits any required weights on its authorized pool. We call this federated evidence-guided search, distinguishing it from parameter averaging. Section 3.3 states the access boundary and its limitations.
