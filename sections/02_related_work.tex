\section*{2. Related work}
\paragraph{Collaborative learning for biological data.}
Parameter aggregation enables institutions to train a shared model while retaining their measurements locally \citep{McMahan2017,Sheller2020}. In single-cell analysis, scFed addresses cell-type classification and FedscGen addresses distributed batch correction \citep{Wang2024scFed,Bakhtiari2025FedscGen}. Tabula and its Chiron implementation support collaborative single-cell foundation-model training through an agent-enabled platform \citep{Ding2026Tabula,ChironCode}. Automated design can interact with local training: FedEx tunes hyperparameters while training shared weights \citep{Khodak2021FedEx}, and Helmsman synthesizes collaborative-learning systems through planning, coding and simulation-based refinement \citep{Li2025Helmsman}.

\paragraph{Self-improving agents in biological research.}
Self-improving agents turn experiment outcomes into subsequent computational proposals. AIDE searches candidate programs through drafting, debugging and improvement \citep{Jiang2025AIDE}, while the AI Scientist and its successor AI-Scientist-v2 connect ideas to executed experiments, replanning, tree search and writing \citep{Lu2024AIScientist,Yamada2025AIScientistV2}. DrugEvolve applies research, implementation, evaluation and algorithm memory to drug-development tasks \citep{Zhou2026DrugEvolve,DrugEvolveCode}. These systems motivate executable revision and persistent experiment history. Our study asks how such feedback can be supplied by multiple data-owning laboratories through a shared research interface. We instantiate that interface with TAPB for DTI, a ProteinTalks-derived efficacy head for proteomic responses, and scDEBART-based cell representations \citep{lin2025tapb,Sun2026ProteinTalks,sung2026scdebart}. These models serve as task-specific prediction components; the proposal schema, local training service and evidence-driven selection mechanism remain common across tasks. The strong performance of simple references on some perturbation benchmarks further motivates separating the effect of a research loop from the choice of predictor \citep{ahlmann2025linear}. Selecting a model specification and its training hyperparameters is also hyperparameter optimization, where random search \citep{bergstra2012random} and Bayesian optimization \citep{snoek2012practical} are standard references and language models are a recent proposal distribution \citep{yang2024opro}; FedEx already couples tuning to collaborative training \citep{Khodak2021FedEx}, so direct search is our primary control.

 BioCoLoop makes this interface the object of study. A \emph{shared coordinator} resolves each proposed design into a complete executable configuration---a model specification together with its training hyperparameters---fits it across the participating laboratories under one parameter-aggregation rule, returns the resulting \emph{development evidence} to a research model $S$ whose weights $\theta$ stay fixed, and appends one evidence card per trial to a persistent evidence set $\mathcal E_t$ (Section~3.2). The same interface then drives two evidence-based policies: revising the next proposal from $\mathcal E_t$ (Section~3.3) and allocating a fixed training budget from $\mathcal E_t$ (Section~3.4). This differs from both lines of work above, which either fix the model specification before collaborative fitting begins or assume centralized evaluation to revise it.
