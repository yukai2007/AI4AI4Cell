\section*{2. Related work and comparison scope}
Automated research systems connect computational proposals to measured outcomes. AIDE searches candidate programs through drafting, debugging and improvement conditioned on summarized evaluations \citep{Jiang2025AIDE}. The original AI Scientist connects ideas to executed experiments, notes, replanning and writing \citep{Lu2024AIScientist}. DrugEvolve combines research, implementation, debugging, evaluation, analysis, memory and algorithm lineage across 11 drug-development task families and 120 test sets \citep{Zhou2026DrugEvolve,DrugEvolveCode}. Our earlier DTI controller comparison used DrugEvolve's released Creation pipeline and local database, with explicit model-transport and evaluator adaptations (Appendix L). The current factorial study instead uses the common Qwen controller and TAPB adapter. The released DrugBAN-Evo module is a fixed algorithm reference, distinct from rerunning that controller.

Collaborative biological training supplies the complementary parameter-learning interface. Medical multi-institutional studies and biological applications such as scFed, FedscGen and Tabula/Chiron use local fitting and parameter aggregation for cell-type classification, batch correction or foundation-model training \citep{Sheller2020,Wang2024scFed,Bakhtiari2025FedscGen,Ding2026Tabula,ChironCode}. Our current implementation uses sample-weighted parameter averaging \citep{McMahan2017} in all three task families; the earlier proteomics local-gradient optimizer is a separate protocol. This established aggregation mechanism is a component of our research interface, not a new optimization contribution.

Automated design can already interact with local-update training: FedEx tunes client hyperparameters while training shared weights \citep{Khodak2021FedEx}, and Helmsman generates and repairs federated-learning systems through planning, modular coding and simulation-driven refinement \citep{Li2025Helmsman}. Native Helmsman and direct generation appear in our earlier proteomics source-search study. Our empirical focus is a common proposal--measurement--revision interface across three biological task families, with controls that distinguish expanded laboratory participation from search and feedback. We do not claim to be the first combination of collaborative training and automated design.

DrugBAN combines molecular graphs, protein sequences and bilinear attention \citep{Bai2023DrugBAN,DrugBANCode}. It provides a native-model reference and the starting architecture for the earlier source-search study. Our recent-family DTI comparison uses TAPB with public frozen protein features and fresh task-trained parameters \citep{lin2025tapb}. ProteinTalks supplies the architecture for an observed-response efficacy-head adaptation \citep{Sun2026ProteinTalks}; scDEBART supplies public-pretrained cell representations \citep{sung2026scdebart}. Matching the task and initialization is essential: published model-family performance does not transfer automatically to our client-restricted benchmarks. Simple linear models can match or exceed deep models on gene-perturbation response benchmarks \citep{ahlmann2025linear}. Those forward-expression tasks differ from our identification endpoints, but motivate strong fixed starting models and non-neural references.
