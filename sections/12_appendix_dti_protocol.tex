\section*{Appendix E. Full DTI search, refit and statistical contract}
\subsection*{E.1 Task and controllers}
We pin DrugEvolve to e945c7d and DrugBAN to 9923f8c. The shared search release uses only the original BindingDB training CSV: exact drug--protein pairs are deduplicated, contradictory labels are rejected, and a label-independent hash ordering with subset seed 20260911 allocates 2,048 training, 512 development, and 512 visible-selection pairs. The supplied validation CSV is excluded. The full final test sets contain 5,505 random, 3,791 unseen-drug, and 2,507 unseen-protein pairs. They have no exact pair overlap with the original training data; the respective cold identities are also disjoint. Coldness means supplied SMILES/protein-string identity, not scaffold or homology separation.

All three controllers receive the same initial BAN module, public three-direction agenda, and training contract. The common evaluator returns scalar development and visible-selection scores to each controller; native summarization determines which fields enter individual model prompts. Thus available evaluation information is shared, but prompt contents and feedback presentation are not identical. The allowed mutation is the complete BAN module; the original graph encoder, sequence encoder, classifier, and featurizer implementations cannot be edited. Encoder, BAN, and classifier weights are nevertheless trained end-to-end, not held frozen. Training uses Adam with learning rate 5\(\times\)10\textsuperscript{-5}, batch size 16, and three epochs; the earliest strict maximum-development-AUROC epoch is evaluated on visible selection. Common encoder and classifier tensors are copied from the same seeded initial reference, preventing architecture-dependent random-number consumption from changing their initialization. A complete model may contain at most five million parameters.

Each method starts five independent campaigns, paired by seeds 42--46, with three new-candidate opportunities, at most 24 model invocations, and at most four fit attempts including the initial baseline. Fits have a 300-second worker ceiling, with a separate 30-second orchestration allowance for cleanup and receipts. Failed calls and fits consume their reservations; identical source/release/seed/contract evaluations can reuse a verified result within that campaign. No campaign receives a free fit from another method. Recorded token usage, successful fits, failures, and observed wall times are reported; missing token usage is identified rather than treated as zero cost. This is a common-ceiling experiment, not strict token matching. The backend does not expose a controllable LLM sampling seed, so pairing controls task-training initialization rather than identical language-model randomness. Engineering canaries and the earlier fixed-model pilots are excluded from these fifteen formal campaigns and cannot donate selected candidates or free fits. A pre-test infrastructure interruption and budget-preserving continuation are documented in Appendix B.

Harness-free generation directly emits a complete BAN source file from the incumbent and feedback. Our harness executes its original propose/apply/run/finalize flow. DrugEvolve executes its original sample/evolve/evaluation/analyze/update flow, SDK tools, UCB1 sampler, and database. An explicit JSON-over-text bridge connects its structured roles to the same recorded model backend. Its task objective is set to visible-selection AUROC: the native language-model judging role remains part of the recorded workflow and cost, but its fitness weight is zero. This is a declared task/provider adaptation, not a zero-modification reproduction of the publication's complete recipe. Native promotion differences remain: our harness and direct require both visible scores to improve by more than 10\textsuperscript{-4}, while our harness also retains its native focus, novelty, and soundness checks; DrugEvolve maximizes the scalar selection objective. This is a native-system-package comparison, not a same-selector or equal-token experiment and not an estimate of the isolated causal contribution of a guard, hypothesis field, or memory module. The legacy DTI comparison contains no federated-search arm; the matched evidence-granularity intervention is evaluated separately on PTPC.

The added cosine reference uses the same final-refit training pool, radius-2 2,048-bit Morgan drug fingerprints and amino-acid/dipeptide composition of the same first 1,200 protein residues. Each block is L2-normalized and equally weighted; fixed cosine 5-nearest-neighbor averaging predicts the label probability with deterministic ties. No k, feature or threshold tuning uses the test sets. This reference was added after the original neural test results and is explicitly retrospective.

\subsection*{E.2 Refit, test and inference}
The final-test gate requires all fifteen campaigns to reach a recorded terminal state and their selected source hashes to be sealed before scoring. Budget exhaustion or controller failure retains that controller's last valid incumbent; it does not remove a campaign from the comparison. Each selected architecture, plus original DrugBAN and the fixed released DrugBAN-Evo reference, is trained with the corresponding seed on 8,192 unique original-training pairs excluding all search development/selection pairs. The refit uses the same optimizer and batch size for exactly twelve epochs, selecting the last epoch without test feedback. Identical source/seed/refit contracts may share an explicitly identified checkpoint across method labels; distinct seeds never share one. A recorded nonbaseline runtime-refit failure, including failure of the fixed released Evo reference, invokes the same-seed original DrugBAN checkpoint as an explicitly flagged intent-to-treat fallback; the intended source, actual evaluated source, and failure remain identifiable, and this resolution is sealed before test access. Integrity/hash failures, failure of the original baseline itself, or final-test inference failure block the final table rather than create a score. The estimand is the controller-plus-fallback deployment procedure, not performance conditional on a successfully evolved architecture. The final table evaluates architecture-search transfer, not reused short-search checkpoint scores.

AUROC on the unseen-drug set is the predeclared primary endpoint. Random and unseen-protein AUROC, non-interpolated average precision, MCC, accuracy, and log loss are secondary. Classification uses a fixed 0.5 threshold. We report mean and sample standard deviation over five paired campaigns, all seed-level results, paired differences, and descriptive 95\% campaign-bootstrap intervals. These intervals are conditional on the fixed benchmark splits, not uncertainty across laboratories or newly sampled biological populations. The two primary comparisons are our harness minus DrugEvolve and minus direct; two-sided sign-flip tests enumerate all 32 sign assignments and are corrected with Holm. Their exact-null interpretation assumes sign symmetry/exchangeability of the paired differences; exact enumeration is not an assumption-free causal guarantee. The practical AUROC difference was fixed at 0.01. With five nonzero paired differences, the minimum possible exact two-sided p-value is 0.0625 (at least 0.125 after the two-contrast Holm correction). Thus this finite batch cannot establish p<0.05 superiority under its predeclared exact test; its purpose is effect estimation and a complete auditable comparison. Small-sample bootstrap intervals do not override that test, and failure to establish superiority does not establish equivalence. No additional seeds are added in response to test outcomes.
