\section*{3. Method: collaborative agentic biological research}
\subsection*{3.1 Research objects and the two-level cycle}
AI4AI4Cell organizes every task around four explicit objects. A \emph{task contract} specifies the primary metric, editable design surface, training budget and data boundary. An \emph{incumbent design} specifies the current model and optimization configuration. A set of \emph{laboratory workers} owns disjoint training and development shards. A persistent \emph{research history} stores one evidence card for every completed, rejected or failed proposal.

Starting from the same task-specific initialization, one research iteration proceeds as follows:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item The proposer reads the task contract, incumbent design and accumulated history, then emits one structured proposal.
    \item A compiler validates the proposal and converts its design identifier into an executable configuration.
    \item Each participating laboratory fits that candidate on its local training shard; the coordinator aggregates model updates into a shared predictor.
    \item Laboratories evaluate the shared predictor on their local development shards. The coordinator reduces these measurements to an equal-client mean primary score, mean loss and learning-curve diagnostics.
    \item The finalizer compares the candidate with the incumbent, records the outcome as an evidence card and carries the selected design into the next iteration.
\end{enumerate}
This cycle makes the two feedback levels concrete. Training measurements change the shared parameters inside a candidate design; development measurements determine which design is retained and what evidence is available to the next research step. The language model remains fixed throughout. Every candidate restarts from the same initialization for its task and seed, so a later proposal does not inherit extra training merely because it was proposed later. Client 0 supplies selection evidence in one-laboratory arms; all ten clients contribute equally weighted selection evidence in collaborative arms.

\subsection*{3.2 Shared inner training pipeline}
At round $r$, each participating laboratory receives shared parameters $w^r$ and completes one local epoch with AdamW. Local optimizer state resets each round, batch size is 64, and the gradient norm is clipped at one. With local fitted parameters $w_i^{r+1}$ and training-example counts $n_i$, the shared update is
\[
 \bar w^{r+1}=\sum_{i\in\mathcal K}\frac{n_i}{\sum_{j\in\mathcal K} n_j}w_i^{r+1},\quad
 v^{r+1}=\beta v^r+\bar w^{r+1}-w^r,\quad
 w^{r+1}=w^r+v^{r+1}.
\]
Setting $\beta=0$ gives sample-weighted FedAvg \citep{McMahan2017}. Momentum acts on trainable parameters, floating model buffers are averaged, and integer buffers use an elementwise maximum. This treatment preserves valid local BatchNorm state while keeping the update rule identical across all task adapters. A one-client run uses the same code path with $|\mathcal K|=1$.

Each full-validation candidate follows a 100-round schedule. Every five rounds, local development workers return scalar metrics; the coordinator retains the checkpoint with the highest equal-client mean primary metric, breaking ties by lower mean development loss. Model selection is completed on development evidence, after which the selected checkpoint is frozen and evaluated with the common held-out scorer. The DTI adapter trains TAPB on frozen public protein features; proteomics trains a ProteinTalks-derived efficacy head; cell tasks train the response head on frozen, mask-corrected scDEBART representations. Only these task adapters and their scorers differ across studies.

\subsection*{3.3 Structured proposals and persistent evidence}
The shared research model is local Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct. Every proposal follows the same four-field JSON contract:
\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}lXX@{}}
\toprule
Field & Required content & Operational role \\
\midrule
\texttt{hypothesis} & One focused claim about an editable factor & States why the revision should help \\
\texttt{experiment} & The exact controlled change and comparison & Defines what will be executed \\
\texttt{expected\_effect} & Expected metric direction and diagnostic signature & Makes the proposal testable \\
\texttt{design\_id} & A machine-resolvable candidate identifier & Compiles the proposal to a valid configuration \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}
For example, a proposal may predict that lower learning rate with a residual response adapter improves development ranking without increasing loss, request the corresponding comparison with the incumbent, and select the identifier that implements that combination. The identifier, rather than free-form prose, is the executable source of truth.

In the uniform study, identifiers address a common twelve-design library spanning learning rate, weight decay, server momentum and an optional zero-output-initialized width-32 residual adapter. This bounded library supplies a controlled instance of the broader harness: it preserves hypothesis formation, executable compilation, isolated evaluation, retention and experiment memory while making the search space identical across tasks and research modes. Appendix A lists every design.

After execution, the harness appends an evidence card containing the configuration, aggregate best development metric and loss, selected round, first and last training/development diagnostics, and the accepted, rejected or failed decision. The next loop proposal receives the incumbent plus this complete sequence of evidence cards. Rejected trials remain useful because they identify exhausted directions and observed failure modes. The feedback-free direct arm uses the same Qwen checkpoint, candidate library, six proposal slots and fitting service, but receives only the identifiers it has already tried. Both modes share an identical first proposal, making subsequent divergence attributable to evaluation history rather than a different starting sample.

The finalizer applies the same primary-metric/loss ordering used for checkpoint selection. Completed candidates can replace the incumbent; numerical failures are recorded and advance the proposal budget. Identical configurations within the same seed and participation setting may reuse a verified completed fit while retaining their logical position in both research traces. Proposal generation, fitting and communication costs are recorded separately.

To isolate whether evaluation history has operational value, we additionally instantiate the finalizer as a compute-matched racing loop. Both feedback-free and loop arms receive the same ten-design fractional-factorial slate, covering low/default/high learning rates and interactions among weight decay, server momentum and the residual head. Feedback-free search allocates 80 rounds to every design. The loop first screens every design for 20 rounds, then promotes six designs using two complementary signals within each learning-rate stratum: the best current primary/loss ordering and the largest round-5-to-20 primary-score increase. Promoted designs restart from the common initialization and receive 100-round validation. The two arms therefore use exactly 800 full-client training rounds, 160 development measurements and ten candidate hypotheses; only evidence-dependent allocation differs. This screening--promotion--validation pattern uses the same proposal fields and evidence cards as the general harness, while making the causal role of feedback directly measurable.

\subsection*{3.4 Laboratory and coordinator interfaces}
Laboratory workers read their own training and development shards. During fitting they return model updates and example counts; during evaluation they return scalar metrics and diagnostics. The coordinator distributes the shared predictor, aggregates parameter updates, builds the evidence card and exposes that compact record to the research harness. Consequently, a new laboratory can influence the model through local fitting and influence the design trajectory through local evaluation while its raw profiles or interaction rows remain at the worker interface.

The current experiments instantiate these roles with ten same-host workers and sample-weighted aggregation. The interface is compatible with secure transport, secure aggregation and differential-privacy layers, which can be added independently of the proposal and evidence contracts. The six-arm factorial design evaluates laboratory participation and research feedback as separate experimental factors.
