\section*{3. Method: collaborative agentic biological research}
\subsection*{3.1 Framework overview}
AI4AI4Cell takes a task specification, an initial predictor, an editable design space and data held by $K$ participating laboratories. The task specification defines the prediction inputs and outputs, scorer and computational budget. Each laboratory maintains separate training and development partitions. A research controller proposes a design, a shared training service fits its parameters across laboratories, and local evaluators return development measurements to a coordinator. The coordinator selects a predictor and records the evidence used in that decision. The outputs are the selected design with its fitted parameters and an ordered history of proposals and measured outcomes (Figure~\ref{fig:research-interface}).

The key mechanism is the return of experimental evidence to the research process. An \emph{evidence card} links a proposed change to its executed configuration, aggregate learning trajectory and selection outcome. Cards from successive experiments form a persistent history, which allows the controller to revise its next hypothesis using both successful and unsuccessful trials. The same trajectory information can guide training allocation: a candidate with promising early progress can be promoted for full validation. Thus the outer loop decides what design to investigate and how to allocate further evaluation, while the inner process fits parameters for a given design.

\subsection*{3.2 Evidence-guided proposal and revision}
The controller receives the task specification, current design and accumulated evidence cards. It returns a structured proposal with four fields: a \texttt{hypothesis} stating why a change should help, an \texttt{experiment} describing the proposed comparison, an \texttt{expected\_effect} identifying the predicted metric or diagnostic response, and a \texttt{design\_id} resolving to an executable configuration. A validator checks that the identifier is available in the task's editable design space. The configuration determines the actual intervention; the other fields state its rationale and anticipated outcome. Appendix A gives the JSON schema and implemented design library.

After fitting, local evaluators return primary scores, losses and learning-curve diagnostics. The coordinator packages these measurements with the proposal, selected checkpoint and acceptance decision. It retains the candidate if its mean development score exceeds the incumbent's, using lower mean loss to resolve ties. Each subsequent loop proposal receives the complete sequence of evidence cards. This separates the language model's explanation of a candidate from the measured basis for retaining it and preserves the outcomes of rejected directions for later proposals. The research language model remains fixed; adaptation occurs in the predictive model and in the designs explored by the loop.

Evidence can influence two different research decisions. In \emph{proposal revision}, measured history conditions which design is proposed next. In \emph{training allocation}, short learning trajectories determine which proposed designs receive full validation. We implement the latter as a screening--promotion--validation policy that retains candidates with strong current scores as well as candidates with improving trajectories. These two decisions share the evidence interface but are evaluated separately: proposal-history comparisons assess design selection, whereas a fixed-slate racing comparison isolates evidence-dependent allocation. The experimental budgets and promotion rule are specified in Section 4 and Appendix A.

\subsection*{3.3 Local fitting and distributed evaluation}
For a selected design, all laboratory workers start from the same shared parameters and fit them on their local training partitions. The coordinator combines the returned parameters using training-example weights, following FedAvg \citep{McMahan2017}, with optional server momentum specified by the design. The resulting predictor is distributed for the next training round. This training pipeline is unchanged across task families; task adapters define the input representation, prediction head and loss.

Development evaluation gives each participating laboratory equal weight. If $s_i(a)$ is laboratory $i$'s development score for fitted design $a$, the selection score is $S(a)=K^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{K}s_i(a)$. Training-example weighting therefore determines how parameter updates are combined, whereas equal-laboratory weighting determines how designs and checkpoints are compared. Every candidate starts from the same task-specific initialization for its training seed. At completion, the selected checkpoint is fixed and evaluated by the task's held-out scorer.

Workers return parameter updates and example counts for training, and scalar scores and diagnostics for research feedback. The language model receives the aggregate evidence cards. Raw interaction rows, proteomic profiles and expression responses remain at the worker interface. The experiments simulate these roles on one host; $K$ is an experimental parameter, with the main comparison using $K=10$ and the sensitivity study varying $K$.
