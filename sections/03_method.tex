\section*{3. Method: collaborative biological model improvement}
\subsection*{3.1 Problem and research setting}
Consider $K$ laboratories working on the same biological prediction task. Laboratory $i$ holds training data $D_i^{\mathrm{train}}$, with $n_i=|D_i^{\mathrm{train}}|$. Designated evaluation workers retain local development panels $D_j^{\mathrm{dev}}$. A task specification $\mathcal T$ defines the prediction inputs, outputs, training loss and evaluation metric. We write the task predictor as $f_{a,w}$, where $a\in\mathcal A$ specifies its design and $w$ its trainable parameters. Given an initial design, a design library $\mathcal A$ and a research budget, BioCoLoop seeks a design and fitted predictor that perform well on the designated evaluation population. The three task families share this research procedure while using task-specific predictors.

Our design library varies four choices: learning rate, weight decay, server momentum and an optional residual prediction module. A \texttt{design\_id} names one configuration of these choices. A research controller selects configurations to investigate, laboratory workers train and evaluate them, and a coordinator returns a concise record of each outcome to the controller (Figure~\ref{fig:research-interface}). The resulting research history informs subsequent proposals. Raw measurements stay with the workers; model states support parameter learning, while aggregate development measurements support design decisions. The experiments instantiate these roles as separate data-holding workers on one host.

\subsection*{3.2 Local fitting and shared evaluation}
For each candidate $a$, workers initialize identically as $w_0=\operatorname{Init}(a,z)$, where $z$ is the study seed. Candidates start afresh rather than continuing the incumbent's training. Let $\operatorname{LocalEpoch}_a$ denote one full local AdamW epoch with the candidate's learning rate and weight decay, batch size 64 and gradient-norm clipping at one. The optimizer state resets at each round. With sample weights $\pi_i=n_i/\sum_{j=1}^{K}n_j$, server momentum $\beta_a$ and initial velocity $v_0=0$, round $r$ updates the trainable parameters by
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
u_{i,r}&=\operatorname{LocalEpoch}_a(w_{r-1};D_i^{\mathrm{train}}),
&\bar w_r&=\sum_{i=1}^{K}\pi_i u_{i,r},\\
v_r&=\beta_a v_{r-1}+\bar w_r-w_{r-1},
&w_r&=w_{r-1}+v_r.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:local-fitting}
\end{equation}
Thus $\beta_a=0$ gives sample-weighted parameter averaging \citep{McMahan2017}. Frozen feature extractors remain unchanged; model-buffer handling is specified in Appendix A. Task adapters supply the biological inputs, trainable prediction components and local loss.

Every five rounds and at the final round, workers evaluate the aggregated model on a protocol-defined set of development panels $\mathcal E$. In the main comparison these are the participating laboratories' panels; fixed-pool repartitioning retains all ten original panels, and source-transfer studies use the original target panels. Define $s_i(a,r)$ and $\ell_i^{\mathrm{dev}}(a,r)$ as the primary score and loss on panel $i$. The coordinator computes
\begin{equation}
S(a,r)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal E|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal E}s_i(a,r),
\qquad
L(a,r)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal E|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal E}\ell_i^{\mathrm{dev}}(a,r).
\label{eq:shared-evaluation}
\end{equation}
The selected round $\widehat r_a$ maximizes $S(a,r)$, with lower $L(a,r)$ breaking ties and the earlier round retained on a complete tie. Its parameters are $\widehat w_a=w_{\widehat r_a}$; the pair $q(a)=(S(a,\widehat r_a),-L(a,\widehat r_a))$ ranks the design. Training therefore weights examples, whereas selection weights the designated development panels equally. The main proposal-search experiments assign each candidate 100 training rounds; training duration is specified by the evaluation schedule, not by \texttt{design\_id}.

For auxiliary-source studies with different prediction targets, shared encoders follow the same update rule, while each source-specific head is aggregated only over its owning workers. Checkpoint selection then uses the original target's development panels. This allows auxiliary measurements to improve the shared representation while preserving each source's prediction objective.

\subsection*{3.3 Proposal revision from research history}
The first proposal is generated once without development feedback and shared by direct and loop search. History feedback begins with the second proposal. For subsequent research steps $t$, let $a_t$ be the retained design, $H_t$ the ordered history of previous trials and $\mathcal U_t$ the untried configurations. The controller, with fixed language-model weights $\theta$, generates a proposal $p_t$ and the coordinator maps its \texttt{design\_id}, denoted $\operatorname{id}(p_t)$, to the candidate:
\begin{equation}
p_t\sim P_\theta(\,\cdot\mid\mathcal T,a_t,H_t,\mathcal U_t),
\qquad
\widetilde a_t=\operatorname{Compile}(\operatorname{id}(p_t)).
\label{eq:proposal}
\end{equation}
The proposal also contains a \texttt{hypothesis}, an \texttt{experiment} and an \texttt{expected\_effect}: these describe the motivation, intended comparison and anticipated outcome. They provide an interpretable account of the proposed experiment. The identifier determines the executed configuration, and measured development performance determines selection.

After training $\widetilde a_t$, the coordinator creates an \emph{evidence card} $e_t$: its executed configuration and hypothesis, selected score and loss, selected round, first/last training and development diagnostics, and acceptance decision. Using lexicographic comparison of $q$---higher score, then lower loss---the research state becomes
\begin{equation}
a_{t+1}=\begin{cases}
\widetilde a_t,&q(\widetilde a_t)>_{\mathrm{lex}}q(a_t),\\
a_t,&\text{otherwise},
\end{cases}
\qquad H_{t+1}=H_t\mathbin{\|}[e_t],
\label{eq:research-update}
\end{equation}
where $\|$ appends a card to the history. Unsuccessful trials remain in that history; invalid proposals and numerically failed fits consume a proposal slot and leave the incumbent unchanged. Subsequent proposals therefore respond to measured outcomes through updated context, without fine-tuning the research language model. After the proposal budget is exhausted, the retained design and its selected checkpoint are evaluated on the held-out task.

Appendix A gives the executable schema, an example history and the complete algorithm. A separate experiment uses short training trajectories to allocate a fixed training budget among a predetermined set of designs. Its scheduler chooses which candidates receive longer training; it does not generate language-model proposals. This control isolates training allocation from the history-guided proposal process above.
