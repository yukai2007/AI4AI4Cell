\paragraph{Sensitivity study design.}
We vary laboratory participation, data sources, proposer and search budget using the main study's target scorers. Laboratory-count studies cover all five endpoints at $K\in\{1,2,5,10\}$ (DTI: seed 61; proteomics/cell: seeds 61--63). Independent-source DTI/proteomics use seed 61; external-source VCC uses seeds 61--63. Proposal studies compare 12 designs/six slots on all endpoints (DTI: seed 42; others: 61) and 36 designs/24 slots on four lighter endpoints (seed 61), with 100 rounds per candidate. Budget effects are assessed within each protocol because the libraries differ. Main-table seeds 42--44 and protocols remain unchanged.

\subsection*{B.1 Number of laboratories}
Increasing participation adds the first $K$ original laboratories and their training/development data. The fixed-pool study instead merges all ten training partitions into $K$ groups while preserving equal-weight evaluation over the original ten development panels. Thus the first experiment adds evidence, whereas the second changes training partitions without changing the selection population. Both use the default design, 100 rounds and the same $K=10$ fit. For cell tasks, repartitioning also changes within-group contrastive negatives and optimization steps. Zero between-seed SD can arise from identical single-batch updates to a fixed pretrained head; it describes training variability on these fixed partitions.

\paragraph{Two-source proteomic transfer and proposal search.}
Lin \citep{Lin2026HDAC} and Ruprecht \citep{Ruprecht2020Proteome} each add one study-level laboratory to the ten target partitions. Their continuous responses differ from the binary target endpoint. A shared masked-protein encoder of width 64 and drug encoder of width 32 therefore feed separate heads for target binary efficacy, Lin CellTiter-Glo response and Ruprecht potency intervals. Encoders use sample-weighted aggregation; each private head is updated only by its own source laboratories. This auxiliary adapter has its own target-only control, separate from the main PTPC predictor.

Lin provides 28 conditions (20 training/eight development, from five training and two development drugs), with 24-hour, 10-$\mu$M protein log-fold changes and the mean of three released CellTiter-Glo ratios; the HCT16/HCT116 alias is excluded from auxiliary training. Ruprecht provides 109 conditions (85 training/24 development, from 17 training and five development drugs), pairing 24-hour protein log-fold changes with $\log_{10}$-nM EC50: 49 exact, 18 approximate and 42 right-censored labels. The loss is squared distance to the potency interval; explicit lower bounds or values $\geq10{,}000$ nM are censored, and approximate values receive factor-of-two intervals. Profiling dose is omitted from model inputs, although source acquisition doses relate to response. The target has 386 training and 105 development examples, with 148 test observations across 92 compounds and 22 positives.

Each source setting uses 100 rounds and an independent two-proposal Luna search. All 12 fits and eight valid calls complete; Fixed and Loop have identical rankings in all four settings. Their AP/AUROC are 34.98/74.75 (target only), 35.01/74.78 (+Lin), 35.50/74.28 (+Ruprecht) and 35.31/74.17 (+both). Paired compound-bootstrap 95\% intervals (1,000 resamples) for the three AP changes are [$-$0.20, 0.28], [$-$1.46, 2.64] and [$-$1.62, 2.49] points. These are source-transfer results without an additional history-search gain.

\paragraph{Three-source proteomic transfer.}
decryptE \citep{Eckert2024DecryptE} contributes Jurkat proteomes after 18 hours at 1, 10, 100, 1,000 and 10,000 nM, predicting continuous metabolic activity relative to DMSO. Inputs combine log-transformed normalized protein intensities, an observation mask, drug fingerprints and dose indicators. Compounds are aligned by published names/aliases and structures; proteins by UniProt identifier. Splits keep all doses of a chemical graph together, with training-only normalization and target development/test exclusions by canonical and stereo-agnostic identity. Of 145 processed matrices, one is an additional Vincristine copy, leaving 144 compounds. Excluding 12 target overlaps, six unsupported names and two identity/structure conflicts yields 124 compounds/620 conditions: 495 training observations from 99 compounds and 125 development observations from 25 compounds. The source manifest preserves exclusions and annotations.

All four arms---target only, +decryptE, +Lin/Ruprecht and +all three---share a four-head model, seed-61 initialization, optimizer, sample-weighted shared-encoder aggregation and 100 rounds. Each source owns its private head. Equal-laboratory target-development AP selects checkpoints, breaking ties by binary cross-entropy; auxiliary development scores are diagnostic. These fixed-design arms use zero proposals.

\input{tables/supplemental_20260923/proteomics_three_source}

All three sources raise AP from 34.98 to 35.68 (+0.70 points); adding decryptE to Lin/Ruprecht adds 0.37 AP and 0.11 AUROC points. Target-only retains the highest AUROC (74.75 versus 74.28). The four single-seed CPU fits take 101.21 seconds; independent rescoring of sealed predictions agrees exactly.

\paragraph{One study per training laboratory.}
Merging the 386 target training examples into one laboratory gives $K=1,2,3,4$ study-defined laboratories with 386, 881, 491 and 986 conditions, respectively. Lin, Ruprecht and decryptE each remain a separate laboratory. The four-head model, seed-61 initialization, losses, aggregation and private-head ownership are unchanged. The target now executes seven minibatches per local epoch rather than aggregating ten partitions, requiring a separately trained target-only control. All four arms use 100 rounds, zero proposals and equal-weight checkpoint selection over the original ten target-development panels. Training participation therefore changes while the target evaluation population stays fixed. The CPU run takes 76.18 seconds; independently rescored AP/AUROC differ by at most $1.11\times10^{-16}$.
