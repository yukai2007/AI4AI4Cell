\paragraph{Sensitivity study design.}
We examine collaboration scale, source diversity, proposer choice and research budget using the same target scorers as the main study. Laboratory-count experiments cover all five endpoints at $K\in\{1,2,5,10\}$ under both increasing participation and a fixed total data pool. DTI uses seed 61, while the lighter proteomics and cell experiments use seeds 61--63. Independent-source DTI and proteomics use seed 61, and external-source VCC uses seeds 61--63. The two-proposer study has two separate protocols: a 12-design, six-slot study on all five endpoints (DTI seed 42; other endpoints seed 61), and a 36-design, 24-slot study on the four lighter endpoints at seed 61. Each candidate receives 100 training rounds. The latter changes the design space as well as the search budget, so budget comparisons are made within a protocol. Main-table results retain their original seeds 42--44 and protocol.

\subsection*{B.1 Number of laboratories}
The participation experiment uses a fixed nested set of the first $K$ original laboratories, increasing both training and development access. The fixed-pool experiment merges the complete ten-laboratory pool into $K$ training groups while retaining equal-weight evaluation over the original ten development panels. Thus its selection population stays fixed as training partitions change. The identical $K=10$ fit is reused by both comparisons. Every unique fit uses the default design and 100 rounds. The cell objective forms contrastive negatives within each local training group, so repartitioning changes the negative pool as well as optimization steps. These comparisons characterize the implemented training pipeline at different collaboration scales. Zero between-seed SD can occur when a fixed pretrained head receives an identical single-batch update, which describes training variability on these fixed partitions.

Target-only, individual-source and combined-source configurations start from the same task initialization.

\paragraph{Matched source--scenario laboratories.}
Table~\ref{tab:scenario-labs-matched} compares four fixed-design settings: 15 VCC training interventions in one laboratory, all 60 VCC interventions in one laboratory, the same 60 partitioned into four laboratories, and four source-defined laboratories with 15 interventions each. \paragraph{Two-source proteomic transfer and proposal search.}
Two independent lung-cancer studies provide auxiliary measurements: Lin et al.\ \citep{Lin2026HDAC} and Ruprecht et al.\ \citep{Ruprecht2020Proteome}. Their continuous response labels differ from the binary target endpoint. A shared masked-protein encoder of width 64 and drug encoder of width 32 therefore feed source-specific prediction heads. Target fitting uses its binary efficacy objective, Lin predicts continuous CellTiter-Glo response, and Ruprecht predicts an interval-valued potency outcome. Target data retain the original ten laboratories. Lin and Ruprecht each add one study-level laboratory. Shared encoders use sample-weighted aggregation, whereas each private head is updated only by its own source laboratories. The common laboratory and coordinator interfaces are reused. This adapter is compared against its own target-only control, separately from the main-table PTPC predictor.

The Lin subset contains 28 drug conditions (20 source-training, eight source-development), spanning five training and two development drugs. Inputs are 24-hour, 10-$\mu$M protein log-fold changes, and the response is the mean of the three released CellTiter-Glo ratio measurements. The HCT16/HCT116 alias is excluded from auxiliary training. The Ruprecht subset contains 109 matched drug conditions (85 training, 24 development; 17 and five drugs respectively). Protein log-fold changes at 24 hours are paired with CellTiter-Glo EC50 values in $\log_{10}$ nM: 49 exact, 18 approximate and 42 right-censored outcomes. Squared distance to the encoded potency interval is the loss. The adapter treats explicit lower bounds and values at or above 10,000 nM as right-censored and encodes approximate values with a factor-of-two interval. These are analysis conventions. Profiling dose is not a model input, although source acquisition uses dose conditions chosen in relation to drug response. The target retains 386 training and 105 development examples, with 148 held-out observations across 92 compounds and 22 positives.

All four source settings receive the same 100-round procedure. A two-proposal Luna loop is also executed independently for each setting. These 12 fits and eight valid proposal calls produce eight fixed/loop evaluations. Fixed and loop selection have identical held-out ranking scores in each source arm (Table~\ref{tab:completed-protein-loop}). The source effect and the proposal-history effect are thus measured separately.

\input{tables/completed_ablation/transfer_protein_loop}

Target-only AP/AUROC are 34.98/74.75. Adding Lin yields 35.01/74.78, Ruprecht 35.50/74.28 and both 35.31/74.17. Paired compound bootstrap (1,000 resamples) gives 95\% intervals for the AP changes of [$-$0.20, 0.28], [$-$1.46, 2.64] and [$-$1.62, 2.49] points, respectively. These results measure transfer from two independent external studies and provide the source-matched controls for the two-proposal comparison.

\paragraph{Three-source proteomic transfer.}
The third auxiliary study, decryptE \citep{Eckert2024DecryptE}, measures Jurkat proteomes after 18 hours of drug exposure at 1, 10, 100, 1,000 and 10,000 nM. Its source objective predicts the released continuous metabolic-activity measurement relative to DMSO. We align compounds using the published names, explicit alternative names and structures, and match protein features by UniProt identifier. Inputs comprise log-transformed measured normalized protein intensities, an observation mask, drug fingerprints and dose indicators. Splits group all doses of the same chemical graph together, and normalization is fitted on source-training measurements. Target development and test compounds are excluded by canonical identity and stereo-agnostic chemical graph.

The repository's 145 main processed matrices contain one additional manually classified Vincristine copy. The uniform standard release therefore provides 144 compounds. Pre-training identity checks exclude 12 compounds overlapping the target development/test sets, six records with unsupported internal names and two records with conflicting released chemical-identity/structure annotations. The resulting source contains 124 distinct compounds and 620 dose conditions: 495 training observations from 99 compounds and 125 development observations from 25 compounds. The source manifest records every exclusion and preserves the original annotations.

We append a private continuous-response head to the shared adapter and instantiate this same four-head model in every control. All original encoder and head tensors retain identical seed-61 initialization. The four prespecified arms are target only, target plus decryptE, target plus Lin and Ruprecht, and target plus all three studies. Every arm completes 100 rounds with the same local optimizer and sample-weighted shared-parameter aggregation. Checkpoint selection uses equal-laboratory target-development AP with binary cross-entropy as the tiebreaker, and auxiliary development scores are diagnostic. These fixed-design comparisons use zero proposal slots and are separate from the preceding two-source research-loop study.

\input{tables/supplemental_20260923/proteomics_three_source}

Combining all three sources raises target AP from 34.98 to 35.68, a 0.70-point gain. Relative to Lin and Ruprecht together, adding decryptE increases AP by 0.37 points and AUROC by 0.11 points. Target-only training retains the highest AUROC across the four arms (74.75 versus 74.28 for all three), showing that the primary AP improvement does not imply uniform gains across ranking metrics. These source-transfer point estimates come from one seed. The four complete CPU fits take 101.21 seconds in total, and saved checkpoints and predictions are sealed and independently rescored with exact agreement.

\paragraph{One study per training laboratory.}
To assign every training laboratory to a distinct source, we additionally merge all 386 target training examples into one laboratory. Lin, Ruprecht and decryptE each remain a separate source laboratory. The four arms use $K=1,2,3,4$ laboratories and respectively 386, 881, 491 and 986 training conditions. Their four-head architecture, seed-61 initialization, local loss, sample-weighted shared-parameter aggregation and source-private head ownership are unchanged. The merged target laboratory performs seven minibatches per local epoch, instead of aggregating updates from ten smaller target partitions. This is therefore a separately trained protocol, with a matching target-only control.

The original ten target-development panels still select checkpoints with equal panel weights, independently of the number of training laboratories. All four configurations complete 100 rounds and use zero proposal slots. Held-out AP and AUROC are independently recomputed from saved predictions, with maximum absolute discrepancy $1.11\times10^{-16}$. The full CPU run takes 76.18 seconds.

With one laboratory per study, all three auxiliary sources improve target AP from 34.41 to 37.53 and AUROC from 72.33 to 74.39. Adding decryptE to Lin and Ruprecht increases AP by 1.44 points. These single-seed source-transfer comparisons vary available training data as well as participating studies, so they measure the effect of those sources under a fixed design, separately from proposal feedback and the matched-condition cell study.

\subsection*{B.3 Research-model and six-slot proposal comparison}
The short study uses the 12 executable designs of Appendix A. Direct search receives untried identifiers without measured scores, while the loop additionally receives accumulated development evidence. Both modes share initialization and the first proposal. Each slot allows two attempts to return the four-field proposal schema, and failed slots consume budget and remain in the trace. Every valid candidate restarts from the common task/seed initialization and is fitted for 100 rounds. Prefixes at 0, 1, 2, 4 and 6 slots are evaluated after the trajectory is complete. An exact fit cache can reuse an identical configuration while retaining its logical training-budget charge.

Qwen uses the local sampling settings in Appendix A. The second proposer is the \texttt{gpt-5.6-luna} service, accessed in a text-only session with tool use disabled, with request metadata and responses retained. The comparison holds task messages, editable designs, training procedure and proposal budget fixed across backends. Service-level decoding controls and parameter count are not exposed by the Luna interface.

\subsection*{B.4 Extended search and task-dependent saturation}
The extended study increases the design library to 36 configurations: three learning rates, three weight decays, two coordinator-momentum values and two residual-head settings. It runs 24 proposal slots for both backends and modes on PTPC, VCC, Norman and Tahoe. Prefixes are 0, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 20 and 24. The settings for initialization, full candidate fitting, development selection and invalid-slot accounting are otherwise unchanged. DTI's backend comparison is the six-slot study in B.3, and it is not part of this extended protocol.

\input{tables/completed_ablation/long24}
\input{tables/completed_ablation/dynamics_long24}

Luna produces valid designs in all 192 attempted slots, and Qwen in 116 of 192. Across 16 mode trajectories, the primary development score is unchanged in the final eight slots in 12 cases, and the full score/loss selection key is unchanged in ten. Each trajectory retains 11--24 unvisited designs. A plateau therefore characterizes finite-budget search with a particular backend, rather than exhaustion of the design space.

Table~\ref{tab:completed-dynamics-long24} measures the first slot attaining the smaller terminal development score within each direct/loop pair, a common target reached by both modes. Luna reaches Norman's common target earlier with feedback (4 versus 22 slots), whereas direct search reaches Tahoe's target earlier (5 versus 13). Proteomics retains the initial design. Table~\ref{tab:completed-norman-events} connects Norman's trajectory to concrete configuration changes: reducing the learning rate to $10^{-4}$ gives an early development gain, and removing the residual head reaches the final primary-score target. Subsequent loss-tiebreak selections can change the retained design without increasing that primary score.


Within this extended protocol, Qwen's VCC held-out Top-1 decreases from 31.50 at slot 6 to 19.61 at slot 24 while development selection improves. This separates the speed of finding a strong development design from generalization of the selected predictor. The complete development and held-out curves support task-specific budget analysis alongside the proposal-validity and remaining-space diagnostics.
