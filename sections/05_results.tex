\subsection*{5.1 Stronger predictive designs under matched starting points}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{assets/strong_start_results.pdf}
\caption{Completed strong-start comparison. Each controller receives the same development-selected starting design, scorer and search ceilings. Dots and whiskers are means and sample SDs over all five fixed trajectories; faint dots show individual outcomes. Deterministic PTPC controls have no SD. DTI shares the same five backbone fits. Bold numbers mark best displayed means, including ties; axes are zoomed. The improved starting design drives the large gains, not an established controller advantage. All secondary metrics and costs are retained in Appendix P.}
\label{fig:strong-start}
\end{figure}

The best tested pipeline combines a stronger predictor with a reusable source interface (Figure~\ref{fig:strong-start}). Development-selected composition retrieval mixed with TAPB reaches mean AUROC 0.9710/0.9588/0.9317 on random/unseen-drug/unseen-protein tests. Relative to TAPB alone, these are gains of 1.65/2.60/4.49 percentage points, with no new neural weight fitting. Relative to the earlier strongest frozen direct-program transfer, the gains are 0.38/0.72/1.59 points. These are single-backbone-fit predictions, not a five-model ensemble. Each of the ten new DTI campaigns retains its shared starting rule, so direct, loop and fixed-start predictions tie exactly.

For proteomics, the improved fixed feature map reaches AP 0.3699; harness-free research reaches $0.3716\pm0.0055$ and our loop $0.3727\pm0.0039$. All five trajectories per controller are included. The paired loop-minus-direct effect is $+0.00107$ AP (exact $p=0.625$, Holm $p=1$ across the two task contrasts); it is not a statistically established controller advantage. The fixed start retains the highest AUROC, 0.7302, and lowest BCE, 0.3741, among the strong-start PTPC arms. Thus the modest primary-AP gain does not improve every metric. The twenty campaigns complete with 141 successful LLM calls: DTI direct/loop use 15/45, and PTPC direct/loop 15/66. Equal search ceilings do not imply equal realized cost.

\subsection*{5.2 Additional local measurements improve a fixed design}
\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{assets/strong_client_scaling.pdf}
\caption{Local-data availability with the same improved PTPC feature map and optimizer. One/three/ten clients expose 44--59/130--159/491 training conditions. Points and whiskers summarize five compound partitions (mean and sample SD); small dots show partition values. Ten-client runs use the identical full pool, so their repeated value is numerical replay, not independent institutional replication. Source discovery precedes this experiment and is shared across all client counts.}
\label{fig:strong-client-scaling}
\end{figure}

Holding the improved representation and fitting objective fixed, mean AP rises from 0.3088 with one client to 0.3183 with three and 0.3699 with ten: a 6.11-point one-to-ten gain (Figure~\ref{fig:strong-client-scaling}). AUROC rises from 0.6901 to 0.7302 and BCE falls from 0.4015 to 0.3741. All fifteen fits execute through data-owning workers and an optimizer process denied raw-array reads. Ten-client predictions reproduce centralized fitting within $1.23\times10^{-10}$, isolating additional training access from a change in the optimizer. Individual partitions are not uniformly improved.

The earlier full controller-by-client-count factorial remains informative: direct ten-client AP was 0.2974 versus loop 0.2696, while unchanged-model AP increased from 0.2431 to 0.2783 with more clients (Appendix K). Training-only CV subsequently gave loop/direct/native-Helmsman AP 0.3111/0.3189/0.2927 (Appendix O). These complete comparisons, including the loop's negative contrasts, show why ten-client loop research cannot be compared only against one-client direct generation. The improved starting design is shared in the new comparison; its benefit is not misattributed to the loop.

The original architecture comparison tests a different intervention: executable interaction-module changes followed by full training. All 21 BAN-family and five TAPB fits complete 100 epochs. Our loop-selected architectures have the highest mean AUROC on all three splits within the five-method BAN family (Table~\ref{tab:dti-complete-overview}). Unseen-drug AUROC is 0.9330, versus 0.9239 for native DrugEvolve and 0.9280 for harness-free generation. The paired gains are $+0.00913$ and $+0.00506$; both Holm-adjusted exact $p$-values are 0.25. The means favor our method, with limited five-pair resolution. TAPB uses different representations and is reported separately, not as a capacity-matched controller.

Frozen probability programs also transfer across backbones. Substituting TAPB for DrugBAN, without changing the loop-selected source or conducting new search, reaches AUROC 0.9669/0.9512/0.9149. Direct transfer reaches 0.9672/0.9515/0.9158, and the shared manual mixture 0.9666/0.9507/0.9143. All ten sources transfer; none is selected by test rank. The useful result is predictor complementarity and code portability, with a small loop-minus-direct difference. \subsection*{5.4 What the loop changes, retains and transmits}
In the fixed first strong-start PTPC trajectory (742), the loop adds a separately normalized temporal-difference block to the observed 24h profile, then tests weak temporal modulation of compound features. Pooled training OOF AP rises from 0.43099 to 0.44993, 0.45008 and 0.45019; minimum-fold AP improves at every retained step (Appendix P). The shared artifact is a row-wise feature map; clients fit its coefficients and return performance summaries, not their biological rows. This is a measured computational change, not confirmation of a biological mechanism.

The earlier first-index case illustrates learning from rejection: increasing a temporal-difference weight from 1 to 1.5 worsens both visible scores; the next proposal cites the failure and reduces the retained parent's weight to 0.75, improving both scores (Appendix K.3). Conversely, a 288-configuration ESM retrieval probe and 183 strong-start cell-policy variants do not improve the retained final results. Reverse-ID/phenocopy/CEX remain at 0.803922/0.723917/0.096127. All three cell tasks and the unsuccessful routes remain reported (Appendices I, N and P).
