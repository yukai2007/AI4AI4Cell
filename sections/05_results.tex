\subsection*{5.2 Separating participation, search and feedback}
Table~\ref{tab:strong-effects} reports the two necessary matched-access ablations and the participation contrast for every main-table endpoint. Search compares the complete loop against a fixed recipe, feedback compares the loop against matched-budget direct search, and participation compares ten against one laboratory with the loop held fixed. The last contrast changes available data, development evidence and computation; it therefore does not establish superiority over centralized optimization on the same examples.

\input{tables/strong_v3/effects}

At ten-laboratory access, the complete loop improves over the fixed recipe by \StrongDTISearchEffect{} AUROC points on DTI and \StrongNormanSearchEffect{} Top-1 points on Norman, ties on VCC, is nearly unchanged on proteomics, and is lower on Tahoe. The stricter matched-budget feedback contrast---loop versus direct optimization at the same ten-laboratory access---is exactly zero on all five endpoints because both modes select the same checkpoints after frequently covering the same six designs (Appendix R). Additional laboratory participation is the consistent effect: holding loop research fixed, it adds \StrongDTIFederationEffect{} AUROC points for DTI, \StrongPTPCFederationEffect{} AP points for proteomics, and \StrongVCCFederationEffect{}, \StrongNormanFederationEffect{} and \StrongTahoeFederationEffect{} Top-1 points for the three cell strata.

The factorial result separates a strong participation benefit from a final-budget feedback tie. An executed-prefix diagnostic over all 15 ten-laboratory task--seed trajectories (Appendix R) finds a narrower efficiency signal: after two proposals, loop search is better in two trajectories, tied in thirteen and worse in none, for a mean development-primary difference of +0.78 points. Direct search catches up by the six-proposal endpoint.

With only client 0, loop minus fixed is negative for proteomics (\StrongPTPCSingleSearchEffect{} AP points) and for the three cell strata (\StrongVCCSingleSearchEffect{}, \StrongNormanSingleSearchEffect{} and \StrongTahoeSingleSearchEffect{} Top-1 points), showing that locally selected changes need not transfer. The complete DTI endpoint table retains unseen-drug and unseen-protein results rather than inferring transfer from random-split AUROC. Appendix R reports every proposal budget, all prespecified seeds and paired bootstrap intervals rather than selecting only favorable comparisons.

\subsection*{5.3 Execution and model-selection evidence}
All branches share the proposer, design library, fitting schedule, aggregator and retention code. The paired first proposal is identical; subsequent direct proposals receive no evaluation history. Checkpoint seals, raw predictions and independent score recomputation connect reported values to executed runs. Model adapters and public pretraining provenance are versioned separately, so a simple efficacy head cannot silently inherit a ProteinTalks label or a target-trained checkpoint enter a one-laboratory baseline.

Synthetic regression tests are separate from scientific model selection. The earlier native DTI failure was traced to momentum on BatchNorm buffers; the common V3 trainer applies momentum only to parameters. Five response-free tests validate the cell attention-mask correction, including causal direction, padding exclusion, unchanged weights and BF16 execution. Every cell arm is rerun on corrected features. Any resulting improvement over the original implementation is a correctness effect, not a loop contribution.

\subsection*{5.4 Earlier task-aligned supporting experiments}
Historical full-training architecture transfer reached unseen-drug AUROC 0.9330 for loop-selected DrugBAN designs, versus 0.9239 for native DrugEvolve and 0.9280 for direct generation across five paired seeds (Appendix L). A stronger shared TAPB--retrieval design reached random/unseen-drug/unseen-protein AUROC 0.9710/0.9588/0.9317, with direct and loop tying from the same start (Appendix P). These studies support task-aligned model development but use different protocols; none fills a missing cell in the new uniform-protocol table.
