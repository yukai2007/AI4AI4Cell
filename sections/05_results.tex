\subsection*{4.5 Learning from different laboratory scenarios}
In source-as-laboratory experiments, each auxiliary collection represents a separate training laboratory. The target's original development panels select checkpoints, and a common target test measures transfer. For DTI, BindingDB, BioSNAP, Davis and Human form four collection-defined laboratories. For VCC, the auxiliary laboratories hold Replogle/K562, Nadig/HepG2 and Jiang/IFNB/K562 data, differing in study, cellular background or stimulation rather than being random shards. Appendix B gives their identities, sizes and exclusions.

For proteomics, we assign each of four studies to one training laboratory: the target PTPC collection, Lin, Ruprecht and decryptE \citep{Lin2026HDAC,Ruprecht2020Proteome,Eckert2024DecryptE}. A source-aware adapter shares encoders and retains private response heads to accommodate their different supervised objectives. Under the same fixed recipe and target-only development selection, adding all three external studies raises target AP from 34.41 to 37.53 and AUROC from 72.33 to 74.39. Table~\ref{tab:proteomics-study-clients} reports all four controls; Appendix B also retains the separate protocol with ten target training shards. Together, these studies show that external measurements can improve target prediction, while source compatibility matters alongside data volume.

DTI likewise benefits from compatible collections: Davis and Human raise target-only AUROC from 85.47 to 87.86 and 87.95. BioSNAP and all three combined yield 82.44 and 84.30. Adding Jiang raises VCC Top-1 from 29.49 to 33.07, with paired intervention-bootstrap 95\% CI [$-$2.11, 9.51] for the +3.58-point difference; combining all sources gives 27.33.

To compare source composition at matched participation and condition count, we fix a 15-intervention VCC anchor laboratory and add three laboratories with 15 interventions each: either further VCC conditions or one collection from each external study. Table~\ref{tab:scenario-labs-matched} shows that cross-scenario training reaches 28.82\% Top-1, compared with 30.70\% for the same-source split; MRR is nearly unchanged (0.5630 versus 0.5624). Under this fixed design, heterogeneous updates do not improve target identification. Matching $K$ and intervention counts isolates this from increasing count alone, while source-specific cell coverage and gene panels remain part of the shift.
