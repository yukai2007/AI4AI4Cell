\subsection*{4.5 Learning from different laboratory scenarios}
Source-as-laboratory experiments replace random shards with distinct biological collections: BindingDB, BioSNAP, Davis and Human for DTI, three external single-cell studies for VCC, and the target PTPC collection plus Lin, Ruprecht and decryptE for proteomics \citep{Lin2026HDAC,Ruprecht2020Proteome,Eckert2024DecryptE}. The target's development panels select checkpoints and a common target test measures transfer (Appendix~B gives identities, sizes and exclusions). External measurements can improve target prediction: adding all three proteomic studies raises AP from 34.41 to 37.53, and Davis and Human raise DTI AUROC from 85.47 to 87.86 and 87.95. Source compatibility nevertheless matters alongside data volume, and at matched participation and condition count heterogeneous updates do not improve VCC identification (28.82\% versus 30.70\%).

\subsection*{4.7 How does feedback change the research trajectory?}
Direct and loop share the designs, initial proposal, training schedule and selection rule; only loop receives measured history (Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct and \texttt{gpt-5.6-luna}, all five endpoints, DTI seed 42 and other tasks seed 61). Appendix~B reports development-selected and held-out test performance at each prespecified proposal prefix.

Measured evidence changes how quickly the search reaches a good design. Over the 36-design, 24-slot library the model reaches the common development target at slot 4 with history against slot 22 without it, and across ten short direct/loop pairs Norman/Luna improves by 0.81 Top-1 points while eight pairs tie.
