\subsection*{4.4 Evidence-guided training allocation}
Evidence-guided allocation improves held-out performance at a matched budget (Table~\ref{tab:strong-effects}). Uniform allocation trains all ten designs for 80 rounds. The evidence-guided policy screens them for 20 rounds and restarts six selected designs for 100-round training. Each schedule uses 800 aggregation rounds and 160 development evaluations.
\input{tables/strong_v3/effects}

Norman Top-1 increases from 11.48\% to 22.32\%, a 10.84-point gain with a hierarchical paired-bootstrap 95\% interval of [4.54, 17.73]. A selected low-learning-rate model improves beyond the uniform stopping point. Across the twelve task--seed pairs, allocation yields four wins, eight ties and no losses: three gains on Norman and one on Tahoe. Screening and restarting costs are included. This fixed-slate experiment isolates training allocation from language-model proposal history.

A fresh 360-fit study adds score-only and random promotion (Table~\ref{tab:rebuttal-allocation}). On Tahoe, trajectory promotion reaches 22.89 versus 20.90 for score-only and 21.95 for random. On Norman, score-only reaches 21.70 and random 22.63, versus our 22.32; random also leads on VCC (22.49 versus 20.01). Selective longer fitting therefore explains much of the gain over uniform allocation, while the choice of promotion rule has task-dependent effects.

\subsection*{4.5 Learning from different data sources}
Source-transfer studies use laboratories from distinct biological datasets or experimental settings. We combine BindingDB with BioSNAP, Davis and Human; VCC with three external single-cell studies; and PTPC with Lin, Ruprecht and decryptE \citep{Lin2026HDAC,Ruprecht2020Proteome,Eckert2024DecryptE}. Target development data select each model, and all configurations use the same target test set.

With one proteomic study per laboratory, the three auxiliary sources raise target AP from 34.41 to 37.53 and AUROC from 72.33 to 74.39. Shared encoders combine local updates while source-specific heads retain their own objectives. Davis and Human also improve target DTI AUROC. Transfer depends on source compatibility: at four laboratories and 60 training interventions in both cell arms, same-source VCC reaches 30.70\% Top-1 versus 28.82\% with external studies. Appendix B reports every source comparison; Appendix E.4 tests proposal history at matched source access.

\subsection*{4.6 Effect of the number of laboratories}
We vary $K\in\{1,2,5,10\}$ by either adding laboratories and training data or repartitioning a fixed total data pool. Adding data through participation can improve performance, while fixed-pool repartitioning has task-dependent effects (Figure~\ref{fig:lab-sensitivity}). Proteomics performs best at an intermediate $K$, and Norman degrades as the fixed pool is split across more laboratories. Performance depends on both data access and the partition used for local training.

Both settings use the default design and training schedule. Increasing participation adds local development panels; fixed-pool repartitioning keeps all ten original evaluation panels. In cell tasks, training groups also define contrastive-negative pools. Appendix B.1 reports these optimization and evaluation differences alongside the complete results.
