\subsection*{4.4 Learning from different laboratory scenarios}
In source-as-laboratory experiments, each auxiliary collection remains a separate training client. The target's original development panels select checkpoints, and a common target test measures transfer. For DTI, BindingDB, BioSNAP, Davis and Human form four collection-defined clients. For VCC, the auxiliary clients are Replogle/K562, Nadig/HepG2 and Jiang/IFNB/K562: they differ in study, cellular background or stimulation, rather than being random shards of VCC. Appendix B gives their identities, sizes and exclusions.

DTI target-only AUROC is 85.47; Davis and Human raise it to 87.86 and 87.95, whereas BioSNAP and all three combined yield 82.44 and 84.30. Adding Jiang raises VCC Top-1 from 29.49 to 33.07; combining all sources gives 27.33. The Jiang change is +3.58 points, with paired intervention-bootstrap 95\% CI [$-$2.11, 9.51].

For proteomics, we assign each of four studies to one training laboratory: the target PTPC collection, Lin, Ruprecht and decryptE \citep{Lin2026HDAC,Ruprecht2020Proteome,Eckert2024DecryptE}. A source-aware adapter shares encoders and retains private response heads to accommodate their different supervised objectives. Under the same fixed recipe and target-only development selection, adding all three external studies raises target AP from 34.41 to 37.53 and AUROC from 72.33 to 74.39. Table~\ref{tab:proteomics-study-clients} reports all four controls; Appendix B also retains the separate protocol with ten target training shards. Together, these studies show that external measurements can improve target prediction, while source compatibility matters alongside data volume.

To compare source composition at matched participation and condition count, we fix a 15-intervention VCC anchor client and add three clients of 15 interventions each: either further VCC conditions or one collection from each external study. Table~\ref{tab:scenario-labs-matched} shows that cross-scenario training reaches 28.82\% Top-1, compared with 30.70\% for the same-source split; MRR is nearly unchanged (0.5630 versus 0.5624). Under this fixed design, heterogeneous updates do not improve target identification. Matching $K$ and intervention counts isolates this comparison from increasing client count or condition count, while source-specific cell coverage and gene panels remain part of the distribution shift.

\subsection*{4.5 How many laboratories should participate?}
We vary $K\in\{1,2,5,10\}$ in two ways: adding the first $K$ clients increases available data; merging the full pool into $K$ groups holds total data fixed. Both use the default design and 100 rounds. The fixed-pool comparison retains the original ten development panels. DTI uses seed 61; the other endpoints use seeds 61--63.

\subsection*{4.7 Can measured trajectories improve training allocation?}
To isolate allocation from proposal generation, we give both schedulers the same ten designs and all ten laboratories. Uniform allocation trains every design for 80 rounds. Evidence-guided allocation screens every design for 20 rounds, then restarts six promoted designs for 100-round training. Both spend 800 full-client rounds and 160 aggregate development evaluations. The promotion rule combines the best screening score with improvement from round 5 to round 20 (Appendix A).

\input{tables/strong_v3/effects}

With proposal generation held fixed, evidence-guided allocation yields four wins and eight ties across twelve task--seed comparisons (seeds 53--55). Norman improves from 11.48\% to 22.32\% Top-1, a +10.84-point mean effect with hierarchical paired-bootstrap 95\% CI [4.54, 17.73]. A promoted low-learning-rate residual design benefits from training beyond uniform allocation's stopping point. Tahoe gains 1.99 points; VCC and proteomics tie. This result supports using learning trajectories to decide where to spend training compute, separately from the proposal-history effects in Section 4.6.
