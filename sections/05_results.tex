\subsection*{4.3 Collaborative versus single-laboratory development}
Collaboration produces substantially stronger models. Running the same history-enabled Qwen workflow with laboratory 0 alone and with ten laboratories, collaboration improves every primary endpoint in mean on the common held-out tests: DTI AUROC 89.89$\rightarrow$93.76, proteomic AP 20.13$\rightarrow$36.99, VCC Top-1 20.22$\rightarrow$31.54, Norman 14.15$\rightarrow$22.25 and Tahoe 15.42$\rightarrow$22.39 (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:single-lab-search}). The gain is not recovered by searching harder alone: single-laboratory random, Bayesian and exhaustive twelve-design search remain below the collaborative result in mean on all five endpoints, and collaboration also improves DTI on unseen drugs and targets (Appendix D).

A fixed default design trained with all ten laboratories separates shared data from collaborative design search (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:single-lab-search}). On DTI, proteomics and VCC, shared data account for most of the gain: the fixed model rises from 82.83 to 91.80 on DTI, 25.05 to 37.07 on proteomics and 29.94 to 31.54 on VCC. Collaborative search then adds 1.96 AUROC on DTI and 6.77 Top-1 points on Norman, where the default design falls from 25.56 with one laboratory to 15.48 with ten; it nearly matches the fixed model on proteomics (36.99 versus 37.07 AP), ties it on VCC and is 3.48 points lower on Tahoe, where a development-selected design transfers less well (Appendix E.3 analyzes this divergence).

Evidence-guided allocation improves held-out performance at a matched budget (Table~\ref{tab:strong-effects}). Uniform allocation trains all ten designs for 80 rounds. The evidence-guided policy screens them for 20 rounds and restarts six selected designs for 100-round training. Each schedule uses 800 aggregation rounds and 160 development evaluations.

Norman Top-1 increases from 11.48\% to 22.32\%, a 10.84-point gain with a hierarchical paired-bootstrap 95\% interval of [4.54, 17.73]. A selected low-learning-rate model improves beyond the uniform stopping point. Across the twelve task--seed pairs, allocation yields four wins, eight ties and no losses: three gains on Norman and one on Tahoe. Screening and restarting costs are included. A fresh 360-fit study adds score-only and random promotion (Table~\ref{tab:rebuttal-allocation}). On Tahoe, trajectory promotion reaches 22.89 versus 20.90 for score-only and 21.95 for random. On Norman, score-only reaches 21.70 and random 22.63, versus our 22.32; random also leads on VCC (22.49 versus 20.01). Against random promotion, trajectory promotion wins six and loses six of the twelve task--seed pairs (mean $-0.44$ points), while it wins four pairs and loses none against uniform allocation, and wins four and loses one against score-only promotion. Screening followed by selective longer fitting therefore explains most of the gain over uniform allocation; the trajectory rule adds robustness over score-only promotion, and its choice among promotion rules has task-dependent effects.

\subsection*{4.5 Learning from different data sources}
We also test collaboration when laboratories hold different biological studies rather than shards of one pool. We combine BindingDB with BioSNAP, Davis and Human; VCC with three external single-cell studies; and PTPC with Lin, Ruprecht and decryptE \citep{Lin2026HDAC,Ruprecht2020Proteome,Eckert2024DecryptE}. Target development data select each model, and all configurations use the same target test set.

With one proteomic study per laboratory, the three auxiliary sources raise target AUROC from 72.06 to 74.13 over eight seeds (paired $+2.07$ points, 95\% seed-block interval [0.65, 3.38], higher in seven of eight seeds) and AP from 34.60 to 36.82 ($+2.22$, [$-1.19$, 5.59], higher in four of eight). AP improves in seeds 61--64 and decreases in seeds 65--68 ($-5.87$ to $-0.23$), so the AP benefit is seed-dependent; the gain comes mainly from decryptE, and Lin and Ruprecht alone leave the target near its own level. Shared encoders combine local updates while source-specific heads retain their own objectives. Benefits follow source compatibility. Davis and Human raise target DTI AUROC from 85.47 to 87.86 and 87.95, whereas BioSNAP lowers it by 3.03 and all three sources together by 1.18 points; Jiang raises VCC Top-1 from 29.49 to 33.07, whereas Replogle, Nadig and all three external studies lower it by 2.15--2.55 points. At four laboratories and 60 training interventions in both cell arms, same-source VCC reaches 30.70\% Top-1 versus 28.82\% with external studies. Appendix B reports every source comparison; Appendix E.4 tests proposal history at matched source access.

\subsection*{4.6 Effect of the number of laboratories}
We vary $K\in\{1,2,5,10\}$ by adding laboratories and data or repartitioning a fixed pool (Figure~\ref{fig:lab-sensitivity}). With the default design, adding participating laboratories, and with them data, raises DTI, proteomics, VCC and Tahoe above the single-laboratory score, with VCC peaking at $K=2$; Norman instead falls from 25.56 to 15.48 Top-1, the case where collaborative design search recovers 6.77 points (Section~4.3). When a fixed pool is merely repartitioned, effects depend on the task: proteomics peaks at an intermediate $K$ and Norman degrades as its pool is split further.

Both settings use the default design and training schedule. Increasing participation adds local development panels; fixed-pool repartitioning keeps all ten original evaluation panels. In cell tasks, training groups also define contrastive-negative pools. Appendix B.1 reports these optimization and evaluation differences alongside the complete results.
