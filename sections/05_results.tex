\subsection*{4.5 Learning from different data sources}
Source-transfer studies use laboratories from distinct biological datasets or experimental settings. We combine BindingDB with BioSNAP, Davis and Human; VCC with three external single-cell studies; and PTPC with Lin, Ruprecht and decryptE \citep{Lin2026HDAC,Ruprecht2020Proteome,Eckert2024DecryptE}. Target development data select each model, and all configurations use the same target test set. Transfer gains depend on the source. Adding all three proteomic studies improves target AP, and Davis and Human improve DTI AUROC. With laboratory and training-condition counts matched, external single-cell studies yield lower target Top-1 than additional data from VCC. Appendix B reports the source-specific effects and matched controls.

In the one-study-per-laboratory proteomic protocol, adding Lin, Ruprecht and decryptE raises target AP from 34.41 to 37.53 and AUROC from 72.33 to 74.39. Shared encoders receive updates from all participating sources, while each source-specific head retains its own prediction objective. The original target development panels select checkpoints throughout this comparison. The matched cell study uses four laboratories and 60 training interventions in each arm: the same-source VCC split reaches 30.70\% Top-1, compared with 28.82\% for VCC plus external studies. Both arms share the same target anchor; measured gene panels and underlying cell counts remain source-specific.

The separate proteomic protocol that retains ten target training partitions also improves AP when all three sources are added, from 34.98 to 35.68. Its target-only model retains the higher AUROC, 74.75 compared with 74.28. Each protocol uses its own target-only reference with the same adapter and initialization, preserving the interpretation of source effects under that training partition.

\subsection*{4.6 Effect of the number of laboratories}
We vary $K\in\{1,2,5,10\}$ by either adding laboratories and training data or repartitioning a fixed total data pool. Adding data through participation can improve performance, while fixed-pool repartitioning has task-dependent effects (Figure~\ref{fig:lab-sensitivity}). Proteomics performs best at an intermediate $K$, and Norman degrades as the fixed pool is split across more laboratories. Performance depends on both data access and the partition used for local training.

Both settings use the default design and a fixed training schedule. Increasing participation adds the corresponding local development panels, while fixed-pool repartitioning preserves equal-weight evaluation over all ten original panels. For the cell tasks, local training groups also define the contrastive-negative pool, so partitioning affects the learning objective's available negatives as well as the sequence of updates. Appendix B.1 reports the two settings separately with their corresponding evaluation panels.

\subsection*{4.7 Development history and proposal trajectories}
We compare direct optimization and history-guided search under the same laboratory access, registered executable design space, first proposal, training procedure and selection rule. History-guided search additionally receives the accumulated development evidence when generating subsequent proposals.

Development evidence changes search trajectories and can identify useful configurations earlier. In the extended Norman search, Luna reaches the common development target after four proposals with history, compared with 22 without it. Reducing the learning rate produces an early gain, and removing the residual head reaches the target. Both modes eventually attain the same held-out Top-1. Figures~\ref{fig:completed-short-dev} and~\ref{fig:completed-long-dev} report the complete prefix curves for the six-slot and extended protocols (Appendix B).

Search efficiency is measured by the first proposal attaining a common development target, defined as the lower terminal primary score within each direct/loop pair. Both trajectories can therefore reach that target. The common target is computed retrospectively from completed searches; candidate fitting, proposal budgets and development selection follow the registered protocol throughout execution. The curves retain every prespecified prefix, and loss-based tie-breaks can retain a new configuration after the primary score has plateaued. These diagnostics distinguish when a useful configuration is first reached from the final predictor selected at the full budget.

Final held-out effects are limited and endpoint-specific. Among the ten six-slot pairs, Norman/Luna improves by 0.81 macro Top-1 points (22.30 to 23.11), DTI/Luna decreases by 0.33 AUROC points and eight pairs tie. The extended 36-design protocol yields seven ties and one decrease. Appendix B.4 details the trajectories, including divergence between development selection and held-out performance.
