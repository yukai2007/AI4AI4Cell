\subsection*{5.2 Does feedback improve the allocation of training compute?}
This experiment asks whether early development measurements help allocate a fixed training budget. The candidate designs and laboratory data are identical. Uniform allocation trains every design equally; the evidence-guided loop screens all designs and promotes a subset using current performance and learning progress. Table~\ref{tab:strong-effects} therefore measures training allocation with proposal generation held fixed.

\input{tables/strong_v3/effects}

Across 12 newly executed proteomics/cell task--seed comparisons, the loop wins four and ties eight. Norman improves from 11.48\% to 22.32\% macro Top-1, a +10.84-point mean effect with hierarchical paired-bootstrap 95\% CI [4.54, 17.73]. Tahoe improves by +1.99 points, with one win and two ties; VCC and proteomics tie. On Norman, a promoted low-learning-rate residual design continues improving beyond the uniform method's stopping point. Concentrating training on promoted candidates improves the final predictor at the same total training budget. The DTI development replay is reported separately in Appendix A because it reuses existing trajectories rather than newly scored held-out runs.

The factorial comparison isolates participation. Holding the loop fixed, ten versus one laboratory adds \StrongDTIFederationEffect{} AUROC points for DTI, \StrongPTPCFederationEffect{} AP points for proteomics, and \StrongVCCFederationEffect{}, \StrongNormanFederationEffect{} and \StrongTahoeFederationEffect{} Top-1 points for the cell settings. Appendix A reports all six combinations. In this original three-seed six-proposal comparison, direct and loop select the same checkpoints at fixed access. The allocation and proposal-history mechanisms are therefore examined with distinct controls.

\subsection*{5.3 How do laboratory count and independent sources affect performance?}
Increasing participation from one to ten laboratories raises DTI AUROC from 83.65 to 93.88 under the default recipe. With the total DTI data pool fixed, $K=1,2,5,10$ yields 85.47, 88.96, 88.05 and 93.88. The other tasks also exhibit partition sensitivity: fixed-pool PTPC AP is 34.59 at $K=1$, 39.19 at $K=5$ and 37.54 at $K=10$, whereas Norman changes from 49.63 to 15.48 between $K=1$ and $K=10$. Repartitioning changes local optimization and, for cell tasks, the contrastive negative pool. Participation and fixed-pool curves thus characterize complementary aspects of collaborative training.

Independent collections can improve a target predictor, with gains depending on the source. DTI target-only AUROC is 85.47; Davis and Human raise it to 87.86 and 87.95, while BioSNAP and all three combined yield 82.44 and 84.30. Adding Jiang to VCC raises Top-1 from 29.49 to 33.07 (+3.58 points; paired intervention-bootstrap 95\% CI [$-$2.11, 9.51]); combining all sources yields 27.33. In the independent-proteomics pilot, Ruprecht raises target AP from 34.98 to 35.50, and Lin plus Ruprecht gives 35.31. The Ruprecht AP change has a paired compound-bootstrap interval of [$-$1.46, 2.64] points. Appendix B reports every source arm and the auxiliary proteomics secondary metric. These comparisons motivate source-aware evaluation rather than indiscriminate pooling.

\subsection*{5.4 How do the research model and loop budget change the search?}
The matched six-slot comparison in Table~\ref{tab:completed-short-summary} covers both research models on every endpoint. Norman/Luna gains 0.81 Top-1 points with feedback; DTI/Luna loses 0.33 AUROC points; the other eight paired endpoints tie. All twenty DTI prefix scores were independently recomputed from the same 5,505 test pairs. Thus the backend comparison measures proposal-history effects directly, separately from the fixed-slate allocation gains above.
