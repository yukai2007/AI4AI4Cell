After 100-epoch full-data refitting, our harness-selected architectures have the highest mean AUROC on all three splits within the five-method DrugBAN family (Table~\ref{tab:dti-complete-overview}). Unseen-drug AUROC is 0.9330, versus 0.9239 for native DrugEvolve and 0.9280 for harness-free generation. All 21 BAN-family and five TAPB fits finish 100 epochs; Appendix L provides all 93 records, secondary metrics, source aliases and measured costs.

\paragraph{Researching executable probability programs.}
With the shared DrugBAN predictor, loop-selected probability programs reach unseen-drug/unseen-protein AUROC 0.9438/0.8849, versus 0.9271/0.8484 for DrugBAN and 0.9430/0.8832 for the common manual mixture. Direct generation is slightly higher on all three splits; the primary loop-minus-direct difference is $-0.000385$ (exact $p=0.5625$). Appendix M reports all 39 final records and the 15 direct versus 61 loop model calls. This separates predictor complementarity from the small incremental contribution of source research.

The source changes are interpretable probability rules, not opaque descriptions. For example, one retained program sets cosine weight to
\[
w=0.3+0.05\bigl(|2p_{\rm cosine}-1|-|2p_{\rm BAN}-1|\bigr),
\quad \hat p=(1-w)p_{\rm BAN}+wp_{\rm cosine}.
\]
It adjusts the manual mixture using probability distance from 0.5. This distance is not an independently validated reliability estimate. A later increase of the coefficient to 0.075 fails the minimum visible-improvement rule and is rejected. Appendix M reports all programs and starting-point controls.

\paragraph{Transfer to a stronger backbone without retraining.}
Applied unchanged to TAPB, the loop-selected programs reach random/unseen-drug/unseen-protein AUROC $0.9669/0.9512/0.9149$, versus $0.9545/0.9328/0.8868$ for TAPB alone. The unseen-drug gain is positive in all five pairs: mean $+0.01834$, descriptive 95\% interval $[+0.01407,+0.02293]$, exact $p=0.0625$ and Holm $p=0.125$. Manual fusion reaches $0.9666/0.9507/0.9143$, and transferred direct programs reach $0.9672/0.9515/0.9158$. Thus the reusable interface preserves useful predictor complementarity across backbones, while the incremental loop-minus-direct contrast remains small ($-0.000371$, Holm $p=0.375$). All 45 new program applications succeed, with no new weight fits or model calls; the 63 reported records include 18 existing references. Appendix M gives complete scores and fixed source-to-fit mapping.

The weaker cosine predictor contributes complementary order information. A post-final decomposition of all saved predictions shows that manual fusion repairs 2.6865 AUROC percentage points of positive--negative pair credit on unseen drugs while breaking 0.8984, giving the observed net gain of 1.7881 points. Ties receive half credit. This explains the fixed mixture's ranking gain without introducing another fitted model or attributing it to a biological mechanism (Appendix M.3).

Increasing data access improves the unchanged logistic predictor: ten-client AP/AUROC are 0.2783/0.7273, compared with one-client means 0.2431/0.6574. The same-data centralized/distributed coefficient discrepancy is at most $3.162\times10^{-9}$. The predictive change thus reflects the available training examples, not a different objective or a claimed superiority of a new federated optimizer.

Both research controllers can exploit the ten-client interface. Direct generation reaches AP 0.2974, compared with 0.2696 for the development-gated loop. Their primary paired difference is $-0.0278$ (95\% interval $[-0.0545,-0.0083]$; exact $p=0.0625$, Holm $p=0.125$). Loop ten-minus-one-client AP is $+0.0112$, with interval $[-0.0358,+0.0554]$. The factorial comparison supports the practical value of local-data access for the fixed model, but does not establish that the iterative controller is responsible for that benefit.

The same full-pool cosine reference attains AP 0.1996 and AUROC 0.6153. All thirty logistic final records predict every condition negative at threshold 0.5: accuracy 0.8514 is exactly the majority-class value, so ranking and calibration metrics carry the predictive comparison. The five full-pool fixed-model records reuse the same data objective; their zero SD is not evidence from five independent laboratories.

\subsection*{5.4 What the loop learns and transmits}
A fixed first-campaign case shows the actual feedback path (Appendix K.3). Starting from flattened temporal profiles, the loop proposes separately normalized temporal mean and 24h-minus-6h difference blocks. Development/gate AP increases from 0.24592/0.25444 to 0.25532/0.34565, so this source becomes the incumbent. Increasing the difference weight from 1 to 1.5 lowers both scores and is rejected. The next proposal explicitly cites that failure, returns to the retained parent, and reduces the weight to 0.75; AP rises to 0.26515/0.35693.

The transmitted design is therefore a temporal feature map, the learned quantities are its locally fitted coefficients, and the returned evidence is ranking performance. Round 3 inherits round 1's source but also uses round 2's rejected evidence; source-parent identities and next-proposal context verify this distinction. This demonstrates how the loop updates its research state, not that visible improvement guarantees generalization. Across the completed twenty-campaign study, 75 successful and one failed fit produce 19 retained changes, 36 rejections and five failed candidates. All 30 native decisions persist; all final vectors reproduce the unchanged scorer.
