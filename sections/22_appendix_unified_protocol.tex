\subsection*{A.1 Research algorithm and optimization}
All task families share Section 3's parameter update, development selection and proposal interface. Adapters define task-specific representations, prediction components, losses and scorers. The fitted reference design supplies the first evidence card.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}rX@{}}
\toprule
& \textbf{BioCoLoop research procedure} \\
\midrule
1 & Fit the reference from the task initialization and seed; retain its best development checkpoint and initialize the history. \\
2 & Generate the shared first proposal without development feedback. Subsequent proposals receive the task, untried configurations, retained design and evidence cards. \\
3 & Map \texttt{design\_id} to its executable configuration, allowing at most two generation attempts per slot. \\
4 & Fit each valid candidate from the same initialization procedure and seed for its assigned aggregation rounds. \\
5 & Select its best development checkpoint; replace the incumbent only for a better score/loss pair. \\
6 & Append the executed configuration, hypothesis, diagnostics and decision, including rejected or failed trials. \\
7 & Return the final retained design, checkpoint and history; evaluate that checkpoint on the held-out task. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

Direct and history-guided search share the first feedback-free proposal. Thereafter, direct receives the default configuration and untried identifiers without outcomes; history-guided search receives the retained configuration and accumulated cards. Both share the registered design space, proposal count and training schedule; Appendix B reports every prespecified prefix. A completed configuration fit may be reused only with identical task, data access and seed, retaining its logical position and budget charge.

Each aggregation round runs one local AdamW epoch per participating laboratory, with batch size 64, gradient-norm clipping at one and optimizer-state reset. Designs specify learning rate and weight decay; the proximal penalty is always zero. Parameter aggregation is training-example-weighted, with coordinator momentum applied only to trainable parameters (Equation~\ref{eq:local-fitting}). Floating-point buffers use the same weights; integer and Boolean buffers use elementwise maxima. Candidates share the task-specific public or random initialization procedure and seed. The optional width-32 residual module has zero-initialized output; the original head remains trainable. Code uses \emph{client}/\emph{worker} for laboratory and \texttt{server\_momentum} for coordinator momentum.

Full fits use 100 rounds and development evaluation every five rounds. Selection maximizes mean primary score, then minimizes mean loss over the designated panels (Section 3.2): the participating laboratories in the main experiment. Complete ties retain the earlier checkpoint or incumbent. Training counts refer to adapter rows---intervention-level profiles, not underlying cell counts, for cell tasks.

Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct uses bfloat16 inference, temperature 0.7, top-$p$ 0.9 and at most 512 new tokens. Its sampling seed is $z+t+10{,}000b$ for study seed $z$, zero-indexed slot $t$ and attempt $b\in\{0,1\}$. GPT-5.6 Luna uses a text-only bridge at low reasoning effort, tools disabled and the same proposal protocol. It exposes no generation seed; labels identify training/search repetitions. Invalid identifiers, repeats or malformed responses trigger a second attempt; another failure consumes the slot without a fit. Numerically failed fits retain the incumbent and remain in history.

A proposal has exactly four fields:
\begin{verbatim}
{
  "hypothesis": "why the proposed change may help",
  "experiment": "the intended controlled comparison",
  "expected_effect": "expected metric or diagnostic change",
  "design_id": "D08"
}
\end{verbatim}
The descriptive fields are nonempty rationale strings; the registered identifier determines the executed configuration.

Evidence cards contain the configuration, hypothesis, best development score/loss, selected round and acceptance decision. Trajectory summaries record the first and last evaluation rounds (5 and 100 for full fits), their sample-weighted training losses, equal-laboratory development scores/losses and last-minus-first changes in both development metrics. Main-study summaries also include the worst laboratory score and evaluating-laboratory count. Source-transfer and repartitioning studies specify training participation and evaluation panels separately. Cards remain ordered, including unsuccessful trials.

\paragraph{An observed proposal sequence.}
For Norman with ten laboratories and seed 42, first-slot D06 ties D00's development Top-1 of 40.00 but lowers loss from 158.98 to 158.94 and is accepted. Second-slot D08 retains the residual module and reduces learning rate from $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-4}$. Between rounds 5 and 100, its Top-1 rises from 10.20 to 51.90 and loss falls from 168.12 to 153.85. It is accepted; the next design, D07, reaches only 45.00 and is rejected. Both outcomes remain in history.

Uniform allocation fits each design for 80 rounds. Trajectory-guided allocation screens each for 20, then promotes two designs from each of three learning-rate groups: the design with the best screening-checkpoint primary score (lower loss breaks ties), and the remaining design with the largest round-5-to-20 score improvement (lower round-20 loss breaks ties). Complete ties follow table order. The six promoted designs restart from initialization for 100 rounds; final selection uses only their full-training checkpoints. The other four stop after screening.

Both schedules use 800 aggregation rounds, $10\times80=10\times20+6\times100$, or 8,000 local epochs. Evaluation every five rounds gives 160 aggregate evaluations of ten laboratories each. Training/evaluation counts are matched; the scheduler generates no language-model proposals.

Section 4.4 reports the paired held-out outcomes; Appendix E.5 gives the complete promotion controls. A separate TAPB development replay has unequal candidate-round budgets and is excluded from the held-out allocation evidence.

\subsection*{A.4 Data construction and task adapters}
\paragraph{Drug--target interaction.}
BindingDB contains 50,149 fitting and 5,604 development pairs. Assigning canonical compounds to ten laboratories gives 4,932--5,058 training and 517--643 development pairs each. Test sets contain 5,505 random, 3,791 unseen-drug and 2,507 unseen-protein pairs. TAPB combines frozen public protein features with trainable drug, fusion and classification components, optionally adding a residual adapter before confounder-averaged prediction. Its objective combines classification NLL and masked-language loss.

\paragraph{Observed-response proteomics.}
PTPC has 333 compound groups and 491 conditions: 386 fitting (35--42 per laboratory) and 105 development conditions; the test set has 148. Each 6h/24h profile undergoes log1p, within-profile min--max normalization over finite observations, zero filling and unit L2 normalization. The 2,048-bit Morgan vector is L2-normalized and scaled by 0.5. Two 5,171-dimensional protein blocks plus drug features form a 12,390-dimensional float32 input. The ProteinTalks-derived head combines protein/drug convolutional branches and a 64--32--1 classifier (464,193 D00 parameters), trained from random initialization with binary cross-entropy. All methods share these inputs and architecture; selection uses AP.

\paragraph{Cell inputs and response units.}
VCC, Norman and Tahoe use whole-intervention fitting/development/test splits of 60/20/20, 70/20/27 and 180/60/67. Training uses one mean profile per intervention. VCC development/test queries average disjoint groups of up to 64 cells, omitting a final group smaller than 16. Norman uses released scVI-derived differential-expression profiles per gene pair; Tahoe uses one aggregate profile per compound at the selected cell context and dose.

scDEBART encodes the shared untreated reference and candidate perturbation; its features are frozen while the response head trains. The corrected extractor implements three additive blocking masks, preserving pretrained weights, gene panels, targets, partitions and candidate options. Genetic scoring excludes the union of directly perturbed genes. Tahoe appends a fixed 128-dimensional projection (seed 1234) of a 2,048-bit Morgan fingerprint to 768-dimensional backbone features, zero-initializing the additional input weights. Training minimizes response MSE plus 0.1 times local-minibatch intervention contrastive loss, with cosine-logit temperature 0.1.

\paragraph{Five-option queries and scoring.}
Each development/test intervention receives four distinct distractors sampled uniformly without replacement from its \emph{own split}, excluding the true intervention. After adding the true option, positions are shuffled. This roster is fixed across its response queries, training seeds and methods. Split and roster generation use seed 20260918. Training instead draws contrastive negatives from the local minibatch, not these rosters.

Candidates are ranked by cosine similarity between predicted and observed responses, L2-normalized over scoring genes; ties retain shuffled option order. For $G$ interventions, $m_g$ queries per intervention $g$, and true-option rank $r_{g,j}$ in query $j$, the primary score is
\[
\operatorname{MacroTop1}=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\frac{1}{m_g}
\sum_{j=1}^{m_g}\mathbf{1}\{r_{g,j}=1\}.
\]
Interventions receive equal weight regardless of query count. Micro Top-1, mean reciprocal rank and Top-3 average over queries. Development loss is cross-entropy over the five cosine scores at temperature 0.1. Selection computes metrics within each laboratory, then averages laboratories equally.

\paragraph{Identification diagnostics.}
Frozen-scDEBART diagnostics span 7.5--31.6\% macro Top-1 (chance: 20\%) and 36.00--57.00 MRR (chance: 45.67\%, from $(1+1/2+1/3+1/4+1/5)/5$). The untrained public head scores 7.5\% on Norman. True-option positions are near-uniform; an oracle constructed from observed held-out response prototypes retrieves 94.5\% on VCC and 100\% on Norman.

\subsection*{A.5 Complete comparisons and uncertainty}
The tables report controls, secondary metrics and all three DTI test sets. Bold marks the best task/metric values, including ties; higher is better except for binary and cell cross-entropy.

Three-seed means and sample SDs describe training/search variability on fixed partitions. Sample-level intervals use 1,000 fixed-seed paired cluster resamples of retained predictions: drug identity for random/unseen-drug DTI, protein sequence for unseen-protein DTI, compounds for proteomics, and interventions for cells. Contrasted methods share resampled clusters. Allocation uses 5,000 hierarchical paired resamples, first drawing training seeds and then compound/intervention groups within each sampled seed.

\emph{BioCoLoop Statistical Supplement}, Tables S.1--S.2, reports all per-seed differences and intervals for the main contrasts and three DTI test sets, including zero and negative differences. These model-conditional intervals describe test-sample uncertainty; several cell intervals include zero.

\textbf{Interpreting the single-laboratory control.} The collaboration effect is defined as in the federated evaluations cited in Section~4.2: the same workflow with all participating laboratories versus a laboratory restricted to its own data, both scored on one held-out test that neither uses for selection. Laboratory 0 is the pre-specified local reference; Appendix E.7 reports fixed-model fits for every laboratory identity, which vary with shard composition (e.g., VCC). Appendix D.1 explains why single-laboratory search, including BioCoLoop, often selects designs whose local-development ranking does not transfer to the held-out set.

Configurations, proposals, development histories and saved predictions support reconstruction; the reproducibility statement details their availability and verification.
