\subsection*{A.1 Research algorithm and optimization}
All three task families use the parameter update, development-selection rule and proposal interface of Section 3. A task adapter defines the input representation, prediction components, training loss and development scorer. The research process below starts from the task's reference design. The fitted reference also supplies its first evidence card.

\begin{center}\small
\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}rX@{}}
\toprule
& \textbf{BioCoLoop research procedure} \\
\midrule
1 & Fit the reference design from the task initialization and seed, then retain its best development checkpoint and initialize the research history. \\
2 & Generate the shared first proposal without development feedback. From the second slot onward, supply the task, untried configurations, retained design and prior evidence cards. \\
3 & Parse the proposal and map its \texttt{design\_id} to an executable configuration. Allow at most two generation attempts in the slot. \\
4 & Fit a valid candidate from the same initialization procedure and seed for the assigned number of aggregation rounds. \\
5 & Select its best development checkpoint. Replace the incumbent only if its score/loss pair is better. \\
6 & Append the executed configuration, hypothesis, diagnostics and decision to the history, including rejected or failed trials. \\
7 & After the final slot, return the retained design, its selected checkpoint and the trial history, and score that checkpoint on the held-out task. \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

In the controlled direct/loop comparison, the first proposal is generated once without development feedback and shared. History enters loop search from the second proposal. Direct search subsequently receives the default configuration and untried identifiers without development outcomes; history-guided search receives the retained configuration and accumulated cards. Both use the same registered executable design space, proposal count and candidate-training schedule. Appendix B reports every prespecified proposal prefix. A configuration evaluated previously under the same task, data access and seed can reuse its completed fit while retaining its logical position and budget charge in each search.

Each aggregation round contains one local AdamW epoch per participating laboratory. The batch size is 64, gradient-norm clipping is one, and the local optimizer state resets each round. The learning rate and weight decay follow the selected design. The proximal penalty is zero throughout the reported design libraries. The coordinator weights local parameter states by their number of training examples. Coordinator momentum follows Equation~\ref{eq:local-fitting} and applies only to trainable parameters. Floating-point buffers use sample-weighted averaging, while integer and Boolean buffers use an elementwise maximum. These buffers store non-parameter model state. Every candidate starts with the same task-specific public or random initialization procedure and study seed. The optional residual module has width 32 and a zero-initialized output, while the original prediction head remains trainable. The implementation calls each laboratory a \emph{client} or \emph{worker}, and its configuration field \texttt{server\_momentum} stores the coordinator momentum coefficient. These names refer to the same laboratory and coordinator roles used in Section 3.

Full-training candidates receive 100 aggregation rounds, with development evaluation every five rounds. The selection key is mean primary score followed by negative mean loss over the development panels designated by the protocol (Section 3.2). In the main experiment these panels are the participating laboratories. A complete tie retains the earlier checkpoint or incumbent. Training-example counts refer to the adapter's training rows: for the cell tasks these are intervention-level training profiles, rather than the number of cells underlying a profile.

The main comparison has two proposal-model blocks. Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct uses bfloat16 inference, temperature 0.7, top-$p$ 0.9 and at most 512 new tokens. For zero-indexed proposal slot $t$ and generation attempt $b\in\{0,1\}$, its sampling seed is $z+t+10{,}000b$, where $z$ is the study seed. GPT-5.6 Luna uses a text-only bridge at low reasoning effort with tools disabled and the same bounded proposal protocol. Its service exposes no generation seed; the seed labels identify training and search repetitions. An invalid identifier, repeated configuration or malformed response triggers the second attempt. A second failure consumes the slot without fitting a candidate. A numerically failed fit leaves the incumbent unchanged and remains in the evidence history.

A proposal has exactly four fields:
\begin{verbatim}
{
  "hypothesis": "why the proposed change may help",
  "experiment": "the intended controlled comparison",
  "expected_effect": "the anticipated metric or diagnostic change",
  "design_id": "D08"
}
\end{verbatim}
The three descriptive fields must be nonempty strings and record the agent's rationale. The identifier must belong to the registered executable design space and determines the executed configuration.

An evidence card contains the executed configuration, its hypothesis, the best development score and loss, the selected round, and the acceptance decision. Its trajectory summary includes the first and last development-evaluation rounds, their sample-weighted training losses, equal-laboratory development scores and losses, and the last-minus-first changes in development score and loss. For a 100-round fit these endpoints are rounds 5 and 100. In the main comparison, the development summary also records the worst laboratory score and the number of evaluating laboratories. Training participation and the designated evaluation panels are specified separately for source-transfer and repartitioning studies. Ordered cards are supplied as context, and unsuccessful trials are not removed.

\paragraph{An observed proposal sequence.}
For Norman with ten laboratories and seed 42, D06 is accepted in the first slot: it ties D00's development Top-1 of 40.00 but reduces development loss from 158.98 to 158.94. In the second slot, D08 retains the residual module and changes the learning rate from $10^{-3}$ to $10^{-4}$. Its development Top-1 rises from 10.20 at round 5 to 51.90 at round 100. Its development loss falls from 168.12 to 153.85. The candidate is accepted. The next proposed design, D07, reaches a best Top-1 of 45.00 and is rejected. Thus the history records both the slower-improving accepted candidate and the later unsuccessful alternative.

Uniform allocation trains every design for 80 rounds. Trajectory-guided allocation first trains each for 20 rounds. Within each of the three learning-rate groups, it promotes the design with the highest primary score at its best screening checkpoint, using lower development loss to break ties. From the remaining designs in that group, it also promotes the largest development-score improvement from round 5 to round 20, using lower round-20 development loss to break ties. Complete ties follow the order of the configurations in the table. The six promoted designs restart from their initial parameters and train for 100 rounds. Final selection considers their full-training checkpoints, while the four unpromoted designs stop after screening.

Both schedules use 800 aggregation rounds: $10\times80=10\times20+6\times100$. With ten participating laboratories, this is 8,000 local epochs. Evaluation every five rounds gives 160 aggregate development evaluations, each formed from ten laboratory evaluations. These are matched training and evaluation counts. The scheduler itself generates no language-model proposals.

Across the 12 task--seed held-out comparisons, allocation gives four wins, eight ties and no losses. Norman improves by 10.84 Top-1 points on average, with seed-level effects of +10.00, +11.70 and +10.81 and a hierarchical paired-bootstrap 95\% interval of [4.54, 17.73]. Tahoe gives one +5.97-point seed and two ties, and VCC and PTPC tie in all three seeds. A separate TAPB development replay uses the seven-design intersection of existing trajectories. Uniform allocation receives 525 candidate-rounds and trajectory-guided allocation receives 520, with a mean development AUROC difference of +0.39 points (two wins and one tie). It is not included in the twelve held-out comparisons.

\subsection*{A.4 Data construction and task adapters}
\paragraph{Drug--target interaction.}
The BindingDB partition contains 50,149 fitting and 5,604 development pairs. Canonical compounds are assigned to ten laboratories, giving 4,932--5,058 training and 517--643 development pairs per laboratory. The fixed test sets contain 5,505 random, 3,791 unseen-drug and 2,507 unseen-protein pairs. TAPB uses frozen public protein features and trainable drug, fusion and classification components. An optional residual adapter precedes its native confounder-averaged prediction. Classification NLL and masked-language loss form its local training objective.

\paragraph{Observed-response proteomics.}
The PTPC source pool contains 333 compound groups and 491 conditions, partitioned into 386 fitting and 105 development conditions. A laboratory owns 35--42 fitting conditions. The test set contains 148 conditions. For each 6h and 24h profile, preprocessing applies log1p, within-profile min--max normalization over finite observations, zero filling and unit L2 normalization. The 2,048-bit Morgan vector is separately L2-normalized and scaled by 0.5. The two 5,171-dimensional proteomic blocks and drug vector form a 12,390-dimensional float32 input. The ProteinTalks-derived efficacy head has protein and drug convolutional branches followed by a 64--32--1 classifier, totaling 464,193 parameters under D00. It is trained from random initialization using binary cross-entropy. All comparison methods use these same inputs and architecture. AP is the selection metric.

\paragraph{Cell inputs and response units.}
VCC, Norman and Tahoe use whole-intervention fitting, development and test splits of 60/20/20, 70/20/27 and 180/60/67. Training uses one mean response profile per intervention. VCC development and test queries average disjoint groups of up to 64 cells, omitting a final group smaller than 16 cells. Norman queries are the released scVI-derived differential-expression profiles for each gene pair. Tahoe uses one aggregate response profile per compound at the selected cell context and dose. These are the units scored by the identification task.

The scDEBART backbone receives the shared untreated reference and candidate perturbation encoding. Its features are fixed while the response head is trained. The corrected feature extractor implements three attention masks as additive blocking masks. Its correction preserves the pretrained weights, gene panels, response targets, intervention partitions and candidate options. Genetic-task scoring excludes the union of directly perturbed genes. Tahoe concatenates a fixed 128-dimensional projection of a 2,048-bit Morgan fingerprint to the 768-dimensional backbone features, with the additional head-input weights initialized to zero. Its drug projection uses seed 1234. The training loss is response MSE plus 0.1 times a contrastive loss over interventions in the local training minibatch. The cosine-logit temperature is 0.1.

\paragraph{Five-option queries and scoring.}
For every development or test intervention, four distinct distractors are sampled uniformly without replacement from the \emph{same} development or test intervention pool, excluding the true intervention. The true intervention is added and the five positions are shuffled. A single roster is fixed per intervention and reused for all of its response queries, all training seeds and all methods. Split construction and roster sampling use the preparation generator initialized with seed 20260918. Training does not use these five-option rosters, and its contrastive negatives come from the local training minibatch.

For each query, predicted candidate responses and the observed response are L2-normalized over the scoring genes. Candidates are ranked by their cosine similarity. Equal scores retain the fixed shuffled option order. Let $G$ be the number of evaluated interventions, $m_g$ the number of response queries for intervention $g$, and $r_{g,j}$ the rank of the true option for its $j$th query. The primary score is
\[
\operatorname{MacroTop1}=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{g=1}^{G}\frac{1}{m_g}
\sum_{j=1}^{m_g}\mathbf{1}\{r_{g,j}=1\}.
\]
Thus each intervention receives equal weight even when it contributes a different number of query profiles. Micro Top-1, mean reciprocal rank and Top-3 average over individual queries. The development loss is cross-entropy over the five cosine scores divided by temperature 0.1. Development selection first computes these scores within each laboratory and then averages laboratories equally.

\paragraph{Identification diagnostics.}
The reported frozen-scDEBART identification diagnostics span 7.5--31.6\% macro Top-1 against a 20\% chance level and 36.00--57.00 MRR against a 46.00 chance level. The untrained public head scores 7.5\% on Norman. True-option positions are near-uniform across the fixed rosters, and a prototype oracle built from observed held-out responses retrieves the true option at 94.5\% on VCC and 100\% on Norman.

\subsection*{A.6 Scope and limitations}
The experiments simulate multiple laboratories on shared compute using public data. They evaluate the research protocol without measuring communication or straggler costs or providing formal privacy guarantees. Model development is confined to the finite registered executable design space, with four editable factors and one optional residual module. Qwen generation is seed-reproducible; the proprietary Luna service exposes no generation seed. Public self-improving agents use task-adapted interfaces with the component restrictions and incomplete executions documented in Appendix C. The study includes no centralized pooled-data reference using the same complete data pool.
