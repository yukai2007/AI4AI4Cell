\section*{5. Conclusion}
BioCoLoop makes distributed biological measurements useful for both model training and research decisions. Its common interface connects local parameter fitting, executable design proposals and reusable evaluation evidence across three task families. Collaborative access improves all five endpoints under the same research policy, and the complete system exceeds single-laboratory references on four. Independently, trajectory-guided allocation improves Norman Top-1 by 10.84 points at matched training and evaluation budgets. Source-defined DTI and proteomics studies extend the evidence to collaboration across collections. Together, these results establish collaborative access and evidence-guided allocation as effective components of biological model development, with source compatibility and task-specific search trajectories guiding their use.
