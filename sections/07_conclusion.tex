\section*{5. Conclusion}
BioCoLoop connects laboratory-local model fitting to iterative configuration evaluation through a common biological research interface. Its experiments distinguish broader data access, proposal history and training allocation. Compatible external studies can improve a target model, and selective longer fitting benefits double-gene perturbation identification relative to uniform allocation. Simple-promotion controls show that this gain is not unique to the trajectory rule. Same-access search and development-panel diagnostics identify where a richer experiment history alone is insufficient. The resulting framework makes these decisions separately measurable and provides a basis for improving collaborative model development through better validation and allocation.
