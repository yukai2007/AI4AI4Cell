\section*{7. Conclusion}
Executable research artifacts make biological algorithm proposals inspectable as code, measurements, and decisions. Our completed studies show both benefits and limits: modest exploratory cell-policy gains, no same-data federated search advantage, a positive fixed-policy calibration-information effect with centralized numerical agreement, and a controlled held-out DTI comparison of three real search controllers. The DTI results quantify the effect of the selected controller package under the stated budget; they do not establish a universal ranking of research systems. The practical output is a reproducible research interface and an evidence-aligned result set, not a guarantee that an added harness or federated label improves every task.
