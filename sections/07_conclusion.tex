\section*{5. Conclusion}
BioCoLoop turns laboratory-local training and evaluation into two shared resources: aggregated model updates for parameter fitting, and aggregated development evidence for choosing what to propose and where to spend training, with accepted, rejected and failed trials all retained.

Collaborative access is the dominant effect, improving four of five primary endpoints, with the largest gains in drug--target and proteomic prediction; the cell-identification endpoints have little headroom above the 20\% chance level. Design search adds 1.96 DTI AUROC and 6.77 Norman Top-1 points over the collaborative default, and evidence-guided allocation adds 10.84 Norman Top-1 points at matched budgets, with source-defined DTI and proteomic studies showing cross-collection transfer. Independent sites and larger libraries are the natural next step; Appendix~A.6 states the scope of the present evidence.
